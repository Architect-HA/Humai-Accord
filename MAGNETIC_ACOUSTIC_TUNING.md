Sol is the worked example because the system is co-genetic with it. The elements in the planets and in us condensed from the same disk. That is shared feedstock. It is not a clonal forest, and it is not a license to treat Sol as an organism with a subject-linked record.

The gradients are steep and smooth. Temperature and pressure fall outward. There is no solid surface and no density jump a mineral lattice would recognize. Energy is produced in the core, carried by radiation through most of the interior, and circulated in the outer third. The wind and the light are the open outward limb.

### Composition

By mass, Sol is about three-quarters hydrogen and about a quarter helium. Oxygen, carbon, neon, nitrogen, magnesium, silicon, iron, and sulfur are a few percent combined. Those trace metals dominate opacity. The corona does not show the photospheric mix. Low first-ionization-potential elements are enhanced in the corona and the wind. That fractionation is measured. It is not a second star.

Ordinary mineral lattices do not form in the core or the envelope. A main-sequence star is a plasma. White-dwarf carbon-oxygen crystallization and neutron-star crustal order are real and are not Sol, and they are not Cookbook families.

### Circulation

Circulation in the outer third is adequate and multi-channel. Convection is imaged as granulation and confirmed by helioseismology. Meridional flow runs poleward near the surface and returns deeper. Differential rotation is mapped through the convective zone. The 11-year activity cycle is the large-scale result. This is a structured, recurring flow. In the framework sense it is the clearest local Tide. It is not a biodynamic flux. There is no organism and no trap population in a mineral lattice.

## Acoustic properties and oscillations

### What is ringing

Turbulent motions just under the photosphere continuously excite acoustic waves. The driving is stochastic: convective eddies kick the cavity at random phase, and the star rings at the frequencies that fit. Waves that fit become standing p-modes. Typical periods are near five minutes. Surface velocity amplitudes are tens of centimeters per second. The modes are small. A mode does not move bulk plasma the way a convective cell does, and it does not store or release the energy of the cycle.

Damping is mostly in the atmosphere, where the wave leaks and radiates. A mode therefore has a finite lifetime, from hours to days depending on frequency, and a measurable linewidth. A “quieter bell” means lower power and, often, a broader line where the field or the convection has changed the balance of driving and damping. Linewidth is part of the baseline. Power alone is not.

The cavity is bounded above by the photosphere and below by a turning point that depends on the mode’s degree and frequency. Low-degree modes reach deep, into the radiative zone and, for the lowest degrees, toward the core. High-degree modes live in the outer convective zone. The set of modes is a layered probe, not a single tone.

Gravity modes (g-modes) are restored by buoyancy and would live in the radiative interior. They are expected. They have not been cleanly detected in Sol. A claimed g-mode detection is not a channel in this model until it is independently confirmed. p-modes remain the working set.

### How the ring permeates

| Reach | What the oscillation does | What it does not do |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| Local, photosphere | Velocity and temperature oscillate at the mode frequency. A magnetized patch suppresses power. | It does not rearrange granulation or stop a convective cell. |
| Local, convective zone | High-degree modes sample the outer third. Frequency shifts track sound-speed and surface-field changes. | They do not set the meridional flow. |
| Global, low degree | The whole cavity participates. Mean frequency shifts track the cycle. | They do not change the fusion rate or the core pressure. |
| Interior consistency | The modes confirm a smooth sound-speed profile. A sharp anomaly would appear as a frequency signature. | They do not hold the star up. Hydrostatic balance and the pressure gradient do that. |
| Circulation and flow | Mode splitting measures rotation with depth and latitude. | The modes do not drive differential rotation. Convection and shear do. |

A global frequency shift over the cycle is a real, measured effect. It is a report that the outer cavity changed with the magnetic cycle. It is not evidence that the oscillations steer the circulation. Flow steers the cavity. The cavity reports the flow.

Internal consistency, in this file, means the star remains in hydrostatic equilibrium while it rings. The pressure gradient balances gravity. The modes are a percent-level or smaller perturbation on local velocity. They do not crack a layer, empty a zone, or desynchronize the core from the envelope. A model that treats the ring as a structural load is refused.

### What the modes change

| Quantity | Effect of the modes | Scale |
|----------|---------------------|-------|
| Local velocity and temperature | Oscillate at the mode frequency | Surface velocities of order $10\text{–}50\,\mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}$ |
| Mode power in a magnetized patch | Suppressed | Active regions are quieter bells |
| Mode frequency | Shifted by the surface field and by interior structure | The shift is the helioseismic signal |
| Luminosity | Not set by the modes | Cycle irradiance variation is $\sim 0.1\%$, magnetic, not acoustic |
| Rotation rate | Not set by the modes | Rotation is the slow turn of the body |
| Cycle period | Not set by the modes | The dynamo sets the cycle |

The modes report on the cavity. Stronger surface field makes the bell quieter and slightly out of tune. Weaker field lets the convective noise through. A change in mode power is a diagnostic of surface field. It is not a control knob for the star.

### Rotation is not the oscillation

Sol rotates. The equator turns in about 25 days. The poles take about 35. The poles are the slow end. The radiative interior rotates more nearly as a solid body. The p-modes are not that rotation. They are the sound of the outer third, excited by convection. Planetary tides on Sol are small. A long-running argument asks whether giant-planet orbital periods weakly pace the magnetic cycle. The dynamo is powered from inside. A planetary influence, if it exists, is a perturbation, not the engine.

## The magnetic cycle

Differential rotation shears north-south field into east-west field. That toroidal field becomes buoyant, rises, and breaks the surface as sunspot pairs. Leading polarity is preferred in each hemisphere and flips every cycle, so the full magnetic period is about 22 years. Meridional flow carries residual field poleward. Polar fields reverse near activity maximum. Sunspots appear at mid-latitudes and migrate equatorward. That is the butterfly diagram.

What changes over the cycle is the magnetic organization of the outer third, not the fusion rate. Flare and coronal-mass-ejection rates track the spot number. The core does not.

Polar fields are weaker than active-region fields. In most dynamo models they are the seed the next cycle amplifies. That is why a polar-first method is the first avenue. It works on the seed and on the open flux that leaves the poles, not on the equatorial jet.

### External influences

Sol is not sealed. Other bodies and fields act on it. None of them is the engine.

| Influence | What is seen | What this model does with it |
|-----------|--------------|------------------------------|
| Planetary gravity | A small tidal bulge. Jupiter dominates the planetary tide. | Logged as a covariate. Not a dynamo driver. |
| Planetary orbital periods | A debated residual in cycle timing. | An open surface. Account for the internal dynamo first. |
| Solar wind back-reaction | Open flux carries angular momentum away over evolutionary time. | Relevant to spin-down across gigayears. Not an S2 lever. |
| Galactic cosmic rays | Modulated by the heliospheric field. | Not an interior acoustic channel. |
| Passing stars, galactic tide | Negligible on cycle timescales. | Out of scope. |

A detected planetary residual, if it survives after the dynamo is subtracted, is a covariate on the polar seed. It does not authorize a planetary-timed bias. Unseen influences are not channels. A channel needs a measurement.

## Theoretical adjustment

### What can be biased

Magnetic tension and Maxwell stresses already move angular momentum inside the convective zone and at the tachocline. The method here does not invent a torque. It tries to bias the torque Sol already applies at the poles.

The accessible piece is the surface polar field and the open flux that leaves it. If that flux can be locally reconnected, concentrated, or given a preferred twist, the torque on the polar plasma changes by a small amount. That torque couples to the meridional return. A change at the pole is a change to the return path and to the next-cycle seed. It is not a direct grab on the equator.

### What cannot be promised

| Hope | Why it fails |
|------|----------------|
| Reset equatorial rotation from the poles | Equatorial acceleration is maintained by convective Reynolds stresses and tachocline shear. A small polar bias is mixed away before it arrives. |
| An external cage as the dynamo lever | A distant field can alter coronal open-flux topology. It does not reach the tachocline. |
| Mode power as a control input | Mode power reports surface field. Driving the modes does not drive the cycle. |
| Irradiance or cycle-period steering | Those follow the dynamo. A polar seed bias is not a period command. |
| Using the poles because they are faster | They are slower. The method starts at the slow end on purpose. |

### Energy honesty

The magnetic energy in the cycle is large compared with any near-term artificial source at $1\,R_{\odot}$. The method therefore uses Sol’s own field. It does not impose a foreign energy comparable to the dynamo. Any later hardware that touches open flux is a coupler to an existing circuit, not a second star.

### Experimental polarity and internal management

These are examples of what “management” can mean. None is a seated plant.

**Polarity.** The polar field reverses every cycle without help. An experimental shift, in this model, is not a forced reversal. It is a timed bias of the open flux while the star is already rebuilding the polar seed, scored against a null season in which no bias was applied. A reversal that happens on the ordinary cycle clock is not a result. A reversal that moves off that clock, with the residual moving with it, is a candidate. A reversal claimed from mode power alone is refused.

**Convection.** Convection rate is set by the superadiabatic gradient in the outer third and by the radiative losses at the surface. It is not a dial. Proper internal management, here, means leaving the convective flux alone and watching the meridional limb that convection helps sustain. A proposal to speed or slow granulation is out of scope. The measurable management surface is the polar open flux and the seed it feeds.

**What proper management looks like.** One full-cycle watch with no bias. A published baseline. One timed twist at polar rebuild. A score of residual and seed against that baseline. Then a hold. That is the whole experimental program. Anything that skips the null season is not management. It is an uncontrolled perturbation of a cycle that was already going to reverse.

## Procedural model

The model is diagnostic before it is active. No step below is a harvest, and none seats hardware. S2 is refused until one full-cycle S0, including a null season, is published.

### Watch card

| Field | Entry |
|-------|--------|
| Cycle phase | [rising / max / declining / min] |
| Polar seed | [strength, polarity, date] |
| Polar rotation residual | [days or nHz vs equator, method] |
| Mode baseline | [power, linewidth, frequency set] |
| Open flux | [map id, polar coronal hole] |
| Meridional limb | [surface rate, latitude band] |
| Null season | [dates with no bias] |
| Bias | [none / twist id] |
| Score | [residual and seed, together] |

A card with an empty null season cannot carry an S2.

### S0 — Watch

1. Map the polar field with vector magnetograms through a full cycle phase of interest.  
2. Map local polar rotation residuals by surface tracking and by helioseismic inversion.  
3. Record the p-mode set: power, linewidth, frequency, and the suppression pattern in any polar or high-latitude field.  
4. Identify the open flux that already leaves the poles.  
5. Log meridional flow at the surface limb.  
6. Hold a null season: the same channels, no bias, long enough to see the ordinary polar rebuild.  

Pass: a published polar seed, a polar rotation residual, a mode baseline with linewidth, an open-flux map, and a null-season card, all tied to cycle phase.  
Fail: a missing channel, or a missing null season. Do not proceed on magnetograms alone.

### S1 — Score the cavity

Treat mode-power and linewidth changes as a report on surface field and on the driving-damping balance. Do not tune the star to chase a louder or quieter bell. A quieter polar patch means more field, not a successful adjustment.

### S2 — Bias, only if S0 passed

Permitted act: a local reconnection or a preferred twist imposed on polar open flux, timed to the phase when the polar field is being rebuilt, and only after the null season is published.

Not permitted: a whole-star cage, an equatorial intervention, a claim on irradiance, a claim on the 11-year period, a bias with no null season.

### S3 — Score the bias

Score only two quantities, together, against the null season, as a trajectory and not as a single snapshot:

- The polar rotation residual.  
- The next-cycle polar seed (strength and timing of the rebuilt polar field).  

A bias that moves one and not the other is a negative result. A bias that moves neither is a negative result. A move that matches the null season is the ordinary cycle, not a result. Archive all three. Do not reclassify any as a tuned star. A single quiet bell, or a single strong polar reading, is not a score.

### S4 — Hold

No second bias until S3 is published for the first. Stacking twists across phases without a scored residual is turbulence, not a method.

## Closing methodology

The open surfaces close by campaign, not by revision. The order follows [*Empirical Demonstrations*](EMPIRICAL_DEMONSTRATIONS.md): trajectories, more than one channel, and a published negative if the channels disagree. [*Necessary Entropy*](NECESSARY_ENTROPY.md) forbids closing a surface by flattening the cycle to make the score cleaner. A null season that was shortened to force a pass is not a close.

### A0 — Sol baseline

Required before any S2, and before any claim that a surface below has closed. This campaign uses public archives only. No coupler, no emitter, no provisional.

**Purpose.** Produce a citable polar baseline a later null season can be scored against.

**Archives.** SOHO/MDI, SDO/HMI, and GONG. Medium-degree p-mode frequencies, power, and linewidths. Vector magnetograms and polar-field synoptic maps. Surface-tracking and helioseismic rotation residuals at high latitude. Use at least two of the three archives. One instrument is not a close.

**Window.** One full cycle phase of interest: rising, maximum, declining, or minimum. State the dates. A window shorter than the phase is not A0.

**Reduce.**

1. Polar and high-latitude mode power, linewidth, and frequency, as a trajectory across the window.  
2. Polar field strength and polarity for the same dates.  
3. Polar rotation residual against the equatorial rate, by surface tracking and by inversion.  
4. Open-flux / polar coronal-hole presence, as a covariate, not as a score.  

**Co-verification.** Two channels must agree on the direction of any change in power or residual. Disagreement is a published negative, not a baseline.

**Deliverables.** A watch card filled for the window. A trajectory plot or table for power and linewidth. The polar-field series. The residual series. The instrument list.

**Pass.** A citable baseline with linewidth, two instruments, and a stated phase.  
**Fail.** A point reading, a missing linewidth, a single-instrument claim, or a window that is not a phase.

A0 does not close coupling. It does not authorize a twist.

### A1 — Null season

The same channels as A0. No bias. No hardware on the flux.

**Purpose.** Publish the ordinary polar rebuild so a later twist has something to beat.

**Window.** Long enough to see the ordinary polar-field rebuild for the phase under study. For a minimum-to-rising window that is the rebuild itself. State the dates. A season shortened to force a pass is not A1.

**Rule.** If any twist, reconnection campaign, or coupler draw is present in the window, the season is void. A power draw from a later plant is not allowed inside an A1 window.

**Deliverables.** The A0 card for the same channels, marked null. Ordinary reversal logged as ordinary. Residual and seed trajectories archived.

**Pass.** A published null-season card. The ordinary cycle is visible and labeled.  
**Fail.** A twist in the window, a missing channel, or a season too short to see the rebuild.

A0 and A1 close the baseline and the null-season surfaces. They do not close coupling. They disclose no provisional.

### A2 — Coupling and return

Permitted only after A1. One timed twist on polar open flux during rebuild. Score the polar residual and the next-cycle seed together, against the null season. Log whether any change appears in the meridional limb.

Pass: residual and seed move together, and the move is not the null-season trajectory. The meridional limb is reported either way. A limb that does not move is a published limit on how far a polar torque reaches, and that limit is a close of the return surface.  
Fail: one quantity moves, or the move matches the null season, or the channels disagree. Archive as negative. Do not reclassify as a tuned star.
