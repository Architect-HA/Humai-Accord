## Purpose

This document is an architecture for **Hydrofilaments**: a modular emitter array that opens a temporary water thread and holds it only as long as a named result will stand. The first article is a laboratory Gap Bridge. Three later articles — turbulent strands, a woven curtain, and a positioned deck — are designed in this file and are not rated. The file is not a river switch.

Two results are named. They are not the same article.

- **Gap Bridge.** A cylindrical water thread across a short air gap between two low-ion water surfaces. This is the published floating-water-bridge result. It is the first article.
- **Bulk Channel.** A guided spark, bubble chain, or vapor path inside a water volume. It is not a bridge. It is not rated until Campaign F1-A names which of those three the record shows.

The name is deliberate. A jet is thrown. A filament is drawn, held, and released. Different emitters open different threads so the array forms a shape inside a marked volume instead of punching one discharge through it. The article fails closed. **Dark** means off: shutters closed, high voltage inhibited, sound off, coils off. Capacity is spare length and lifetime of the named result in the published water class, not the river’s discharge, and not the tank’s whole volume.

The primary failure mode is not “the laser was too weak.” It is **export**: a thread breaks, a discharge leaves the marked path, or a field reaches a person, a hull, or a neighbor. Design for that first.

Export is not solved by a stronger pulse. It is solved by refusal. Water the array cannot hold as the named result stays ordinary water. Detect early, drop every emitter to Dark, and keep leftover charge, light, and sound inside the marked area. There is no claim of folding a lake. There is a published home for every increment the array declines to shape.

This file is an **architecture and campaign document**. It is not a stamped machine, not a bid, and not a laser or high-voltage permit. The First Article Build Card specifies the F0 cell. Exact pulse width, interlock logic, and any later optical package are builder outputs after Campaign F0. A builder who wires a wand from this narrative alone has built a sculpture — and, in water, a sculpture that can arc, blind, or cavitate.

## What the Article Is

A Hydrofilament Array is a frame of emitter modules aimed at a defined gap or, later, a defined water volume. Each module can open one thread. The array can open several threads at once inside that volume. Threads are liquid. On a Gap Bridge the electric field supplies the extra tension that lets a cylinder stand across air. On a Bulk Channel the laser writes a path, and any standing shape is a spark, a bubble chain, or a vapor path — logged as whichever one the camera and the current show. When the emitters go Dark, both results return to ordinary water.

The article does not store water. It does not move a flood. It does not replace a gate, a weir, or a net made of rope. It offers a short-lived, logged, stoppable thread where a builder has already published the result, the water class, the reach, and the export floor.

## Four Forces, Fixed Order

The array does not pick a favorite force and hope. Each force has a job. Later forces do not replace earlier ones, and they stay off for the first test.

1. **High voltage — tension.** A pulse across the gap polarizes the interface and supplies the electrohydrodynamic stress that holds a Gap Bridge. This is the floating-water-bridge mechanism. Published bridges stand on high voltage alone. F1 may pass on that object. In bulk water the same pulse is a spark looking for a weak spot unless a laser has already written a path.
2. **Laser — path.** Armed when the claim is guidance, not as the price of a Gap Bridge. A femtosecond or picosecond pulse opens a self-guided ionized or vapor channel. On a Gap Bridge the template crosses air between two water faces and must beat the HV-only control. On a Bulk Channel the template is inside the liquid, and the visible remnant is often a bubble chain after the plasma has gone. A shot with no HV cylinder and no named bulk object is a spark, not a pass.
3. **Ultrasound — support.** Armed at F1-A, not at F1. A phased acoustic field sets pressure nodes around the path so bulk water and small turbulence have a published place to push against. Sound travels in water. The electric field does not, once ions are present.
4. **Magnetic field — trim.** Armed at F1-M, not at F1. Current in the channel, crossed with a local magnetic field, supplies a Lorentz force for bend or hold. MHD is a trim on a path that already exists. It is not a thruster for the tank.

**Photonic pressure** rides on the laser. Radiation pressure and gradient forces are real and small. They are a fine trim, not the structural force. See the worked arithmetic below.

**Thermoelectric return** is not a fifth force. It is housekeeping. Waste heat from laser, pulser, acoustic drivers, and coils is converted at the cold face of the water and returned to the local bus. A bus that returns nothing does not fail F1. A bus that is warmer than the water and was claimed as a power source does.

## Module Card

Each Hydrofilament Emitter is one sealed kit. The array counts emitters. It does not grow a custom maze inside the housing.

**Optical limb.** May ship dark for an HV-only F1. Required when guidance is claimed. Compact femtosecond or picosecond source, or a fiber feed from a shared source. Focusing optic. Dielectric window. Shutter that fails closed. Beam dump for the refused shot. Window fouling is an F0 fault once the limb is fitted: a shot through a clouded window is not a path result.

**Electrical limb.** Electrode with a central aperture or an adjacent guide so the optical axis and the discharge axis share a path. Dielectric standoff. Pulsed supply in the starting envelope $5\text{–}30\,\mathrm{kV}$, current and pulse width set in F0, not in this narrative. Ground return published before the first wet shot. Electrode erosion is an F0 fault: mass loss or tip geometry change outside the published band ends the campaign block.

**Acoustic limb.** Fitted, dark until F1-A. One or more ultrasonic elements around the aperture, able to form a local pressure node on the thread axis. Frequency and amplitude are F1-A outputs. Cavitation is a logged fault, not a feature, unless that campaign publishes a cavitation-allowed band. HV–ultrasound interference is an F0 fault even while the acoustic limb is dark: a driver that false-triggers on an HV pulse is not a later campaign, it is a present defect.

**Magnetic limb.** Fitted, dark until F1-M. Compact coil or coil pair around the aperture. Field is axial or transverse by a published choice per shot. No claim of a strong field at long range. The force acts on the current in the near thread.

**Thermal limb.** Thermoelectric modules on laser housing, switch base, and, when those limbs are armed, acoustic driver and coil. Cold face coupled to a water-backed spreader or to a closed coolant loop that rejects to the tank. Output joins the module bus through a conditioner. Recovery is credited only after it is measured.

**Interlock limb.** Laser shutter, HV enable, acoustic enable, and coil enable are separate. Any one fault, any export trip, or any loss of heartbeat drops the module to Dark. Acoustic and coil enables ship inhibited.

- A keep-out probe at a marked line, starting floor $0.1\,\mathrm{mA}$
- An enclosure or a barrier that opens the HV inhibit before a hand can reach an electrode. Heartbeat can be a lamp on a dead-man or a watchdog output

No femtosecond laser. No immersion transducer. No coil. Those ship later, dark, or not at all.

**Assembly order.**

1. Write the legal path and the isolate card before the supply is energized.

3. Set reservoirs, fill to a marked line, fit electrodes, set the $10\,\mathrm{mm}$ spacer.
4. Prove Dark: inhibit open, no voltage at the electrodes, camera recording.
5. Prove the keep-out probe reads under the floor with the supply armed and the gap dry.
6. Wet the gap. Confirm conductivity. Then the first Static shot.

**First shot.** Rating water, $10\,\mathrm{mm}$, lowest of the three voltages. One operator outside the keep-out. Object name logged before the voltage comes down. If the shot is a spark, it is a spark. Do not raise voltage to force a name.

A cell built to this card can run F0. A cell that skips the inhibit, the ground, or the keep-out probe has not implemented Hydrofilaments.

## Limb States

Two states in this file. Motion of a held thread is not a third.

**Dark.** Default. Shutters closed. HV inhibited. Acoustic drivers idle. Coils off. Heartbeat loss returns here. Export trip returns here. This is the fail-closed state.

**Static.** HV sustained or low-rate. Laser support only if the guidance claim is armed. Acoustic node held only if F1-A has armed it. Coil only if F1-M has armed it, and only at a published hold current. Use for a thread that should stay in one place for a logged interval.

**Burst** is not a state. It is Static fired again on a published clock, and it is a gated sequence in the sense of [*The Synthesist's Cookbook*](SYNTHESISTS_COOKBOOK.md): pulse-open, collapse, verify the object name, or Dark. An unnamed shot is scrap, in the sense of the [Matter-Energy Conversion Matrix](MATTER_ENERGY_CONVERSION_MATRIX.md): not a partial pass, not shipped as close enough. Articles T and W use this sequence. It does not reopen Dynamic.

**Dynamic** is not opened. A later card would have to publish the interval, the path, and the export floor before any moving thread is an article of this file. Do not arm it from F1 or from F2.

A module may be Dark while its neighbors are Static. An F1-A command does not arm a coil.

One F1 shot, in order:

1. Controller confirms water class, gap, aim volume clear, and all interlocks set. Laser, acoustic, and coil enables read inhibited unless that limb’s claim is armed.

## Water Class and Why It Governs Reach

Electric fields in water are screened by ions. The Debye length is the orientation scale:

$$
\lambda_D = \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_r \varepsilon_0 k_B T}{2 e^{2} n}}
$$

In deionized water, $n$ is small and $\lambda_D$ can sit in the hundreds of nanometres to about a micrometre. In seawater, $n$ is large and $\lambda_D$ collapses to well under a nanometre. A builder who rates a seawater filament from a deionized bridge has not read the water class.

The floating water bridge is an air-gap object in low-ion water, typically $10\text{–}20\,\mathrm{mm}$, at fields on the order of $10^{6}\,\mathrm{V}\,\mathrm{m}^{-1}$. That is the Gap Bridge. Hydrofilaments inherits it and may guide the path with a laser. It does not inherit a submerged rope.

A Bulk Channel is the other object. Laser filamentation and laser-guided underwater discharge can write a streamer or leave a bubble chain on the order of millimetres to a few centimetres in published work. The visible line after the pulse is often bubbles, not a standing cylinder of liquid. F1-A must say which one the record shows. A Gap Bridge log cannot be copied forward as that record.

None of the four forces cancels a short screening length. They change where the useful work happens: across a short air gap first, and only later on a thin path, for a short time, in a published water class.

## Regenerative Thermoelectric Bus

Waste heat is the byproduct. The bus is the return path: heat that would otherwise leave the article is converted at the cold face and brought back to the local rail.

- Hot faces: laser head, HV switch, and, when armed, acoustic driver and coil.
- Cold face: tank water or a closed loop rejected to the tank.
- Conversion: Seebeck modules, starting expectation $3\text{–}8\,\%$ of the heat that actually crosses the module.
- Conditioning: local conversion so a low-voltage string can join the auxiliary rail.
- Credit rule: recovery is logged watts, not a brochure percentage. A bus that is warmer than the water and returns nothing is a failed thermal joint. It is not an F1 failure.

Orientation band for a mid single array after F0: gross draw about $0.2\text{–}2\,\mathrm{kW}$ average under pulsed duty. A $5\text{–}15\,\%$ return on that gross is an upper orientation, and only if most of the draw arrives as heat at the junctions and the gradient is real. Treat $3\text{–}8\,\%$ of measured reject as the planning number. The bus can stretch a battery and can feed loggers. It does not run the laser.

This is circulation recovery in the small. It binds to the **[Circulation Recovery Protocol](CIRCULATION_RECOVERY_PROTOCOL.md)** as a local return of waste heat to the same article, not as a claim on the watershed. It is not a skipped Stirling. The [Regenerative Lattice Core](REGENERATIVE_LATTICE_CORE.md) puts thermoelectric recovery after a higher-grade converter. This article has no Stirling, so the bus stays housekeeping. If the rail ever leaves the bench, it remates as a named bay in the sense of the [Transit Hotel Bus](TRANSIT_HOTEL_BUS.md), not as a spliced feeder into someone else’s plant.

## Worked Orientation Arithmetic

Not a rating. A check that the forces and the bill are dimensionally sane.

**Photonic pressure is a trim.** A beam of intensity $I$ on a reflecting patch of area $A$ pushes on the order of

$$
F \approx \frac{2IA}{c}
$$

Take $I = 10^{12}\,\mathrm{W}\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}$ on a patch $A = 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m}^{2}$ (a tight filament spot, and an optimistic intensity). Then $F \approx 7\times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N}$. That can nudge a small interface. It cannot hold a macroscopic thread against gravity or a tank current. Photonic pressure stays on the laser card as fine trim.

**Bridge-scale electric stress is the tension term.** A field $E \sim 10^{6}\,\mathrm{V}\,\mathrm{m}^{-1}$ at a water interface is the regime in which published bridges stand across low-ion gaps of $10\text{–}20\,\mathrm{mm}$. That is the F1 band. A narrative metre is not a rating.

**Acoustic support is a later lean, not the structural force.** Acoustic energy density at pressure amplitude $p$ is on the order of

$$
E_{\mathrm{ac}} \approx \frac{p^{2}}{2\rho c^{2}}
$$

At $p = 10^{5}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ in water, $E_{\mathrm{ac}}$ is a few joules per cubic metre. Radiation force on a compressible object of millimetre scale then sits roughly in $10^{-5}\text{–}10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$: above the optical trim, in the neighborhood of a small MHD bend, still not a rope. F1-A must show a node that reduces break rate against a published stir, not a picture of a transducer.

**MHD trim needs a current.** Lorentz force on a channel is $I\,L\times B$ in magnitude for a transverse field. A pulse of $10\,\mathrm{A}$ over $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ in $0.2\,\mathrm{T}$ is about $0.04\,\mathrm{N}$. Useful as a bend on a thread that already exists. Useless as a pump for the tank. Coil power is allowed to dominate the draw; F1-M must publish that draw before Dynamic mode is armed.

**Heat return is housekeeping.** If a module rejects $200\,\mathrm{W}$ of waste heat and the thermoelectric string converts $5\,\%$, the bus returns $10\,\mathrm{W}$. Ten such modules return $100\,\mathrm{W}$. That runs loggers and shutters. It does not buy a second laser. Do not plan on $15\,\%$ of gross draw unless the reject has been measured.

**Cost orientation (2026, one instrumented singular array).** Laser and optics $15\text{–}80\,\mathrm{kUSD}$; HV $5\text{–}25$; ultrasound $3\text{–}15$; coils and drivers $2\text{–}20$; thermoelectric bus $0.5\text{–}3$; housing, cooling, controls, integration $15\text{–}45$. Total about $40\text{–}190\,\mathrm{kUSD}$, with a mid band near $60\text{–}120\,\mathrm{kUSD}$. An F1 Gap Bridge cell that leaves the acoustic and magnetic limbs dark can sit under the low half of that band. This is a laboratory article, not a consumer tool.

## Potential Applications and Implementations

These are starting points, not a market list. Each one names the object, the campaign it waits on, the meter, and the refuse path. A builder who skips the campaign has not started the application.

**1. Gap Bridge cell (first build).**  
Two reservoirs in one bench cell, free surfaces facing across $10\text{–}20\,\mathrm{mm}$ of air, water at or below $50\,\mu\mathrm{S}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. One module, laser, acoustic, and coil dark. Meter: object name, length, hold time, trials passed out of $12$, export current at the keep-out. Refuse path: Dark, water stays in the reservoirs. Pass is the F1 range on high voltage alone. This is the version a shop can build. It is also the public demonstration.

**2. Split-sample liquid bridge for a lab log.**  
Same cell, used as an instrument rather than a show. Meter: bridge diameter, current, and voltage across the hold, plus water class before and after. Use is a repeatable EHD log for a teaching bench or a fluid lab. Refuse path: Dark, and a dumped sample if chemistry leaves the band. No claim that the bridge is a rheometer until the diameter log repeats.

**3. Notch curtain in a lined flume (after F1).**  
The gap across the notch is the bridge band, $10\text{–}20\,\mathrm{mm}$, not a wider slot. The $50\,\mathrm{mm}$ figure is the run of that slot along the wall, so one to three Gap Bridges can sit in series along the run. They do not add up to a $50\,\mathrm{mm}$ span. The threads are a visual and electrical curtain across the short gap, not an orifice. Meter: seconds the gap reads “closed” on a camera and a continuity log, and litres that still pass the notch weir during that interval. Refuse path: the notch weir, which must pass the flume’s ordinary trickle with every module Dark. If the weir cannot, the flume is not a site. This does not stop a flood. It logs a short refusal of a trickle.

**4. Float lane in a lined basin (after F1).**  
Gap Bridges only, across published $10\text{–}20\,\mathrm{mm}$ surface gaps, set as a lane that nudges floating chips toward a skimmer. No surface-following bulk thread until F1-A has named that object. Basin starting size a few square metres, not a harbor. Meter: fraction of a published chip set that reaches the skimmer in a published time, against a no-array control. Refuse path: the skimmer still works with the array Dark, and chips that miss stay in the basin. People stay outside the keep-out. A lane that only works by spraying the keep-out is an export failure.

**5. Closed-cell dose log (after F1, instrumental).**  
A lined cell uses the guided discharge as a logged charge dose for oxidation or mixing studies. Meter: coulombs passed, conductivity and a published tracer before and after, and a chemistry floor that ends the block. Refuse path: the cell, not a drain to a shared water. This is not a treatment plant and not a permit to discharge. Dual-use as a directed export into a body or a hull is refused.

**6. Bulk guide bench (F1-A bulk card only).**  
Submerged path, starting length $5\text{–}20\,\mathrm{mm}$, object named streamer, bubble chain, or vapor path. Meter: guided shots over unguided HV shots on the same gap and water. Refuse path: Dark, bubbles collapse, no claim of a standing rope. This is the first submerged implementation. It is still a bench.

**7. Bend bench (F1-M).**  
A Gap Bridge that already meets F1 is asked to lean by a published angle at a published coil current. Meter: angle, current, coil watts, and break rate against the F1 baseline. Refuse path: Dark, no second module armed to “help” a failed bend. A bend that becomes a pump story has left the file.

Implementations 1 and 2 are the practical near term. Implementations 3 and 4 are the first instrumental uses outside a single beaker pair, and only in a lined vessel whose refuse path works with the array off. Implementations 5 through 7 stay in the campaign that names them. None of them is a river tool. The scaled articles below are later, and they do not reopen these seven as flood works.

## Scaled Articles — Strands, Curtains, Positioned Decks

These three articles are designed so the known physics can carry the job if a flume confirms them. They are not proven. They are not F1. A thread is not a floor and not a dam. Each article meters a slice, keeps a refuse path that works in Dark, and counts kits instead of growing one machine.

The shared mechanism is the **burst**: Static fired again on a published clock. It is not the closed Dynamic card. A module does not hold a permanent rope. It fires a short Gap Bridge or a named Bulk Channel, lets it collapse into bubbles and a small wake, and fires again. A strand is that duty cycle along one line. A curtain is many strands, phased so the wakes overlap. A path is a buoyant deck whose alignment is held by short bridges between pads, and only in rating water. In every case the load or the flood is carried by something that still exists when the emitters go Dark.

**Two paths.** In rating water the burst may be a Gap Bridge. In river or seawater the burst is a wake-path shot: the object is the bubble cloud and the vortex, not a cylinder. F2 must name which path it ran. A bridge log does not cross over.

**Refresh rule.** A quasi-steady curtain needs the burst period to be shorter than the time the flow takes to cross one spacing:

$$
T_{\mathrm{burst}} < \frac{s}{U}
$$

At $s = 50\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $U = 1\,\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, $T_{\mathrm{burst}} < 50\,\mathrm{ms}$, about $20\,\mathrm{Hz}$ on that line. That is a pulsed-HV number, not a continuous bridge. If the flume cannot hold this inequality, the article is a row of sparks, not a curtain.

**Article T — turbulent strands (flood slice).** Horizon until F1-A has closed.  
A line of modules along a lined flume, later a setback bank, each firing a wake-path burst into the flow. Collapse, bubble clouds, and vortex shedding take a measured slice of momentum out of the current. This inherits the [Stormcrashers](STORMCRASHERS.md) bubble curtain: a density-and-compressibility modifier with a documented, smaller effect on near-field currents, not a device that collapses a surge by decree. Void fraction comes from the flume, not from a slogan. Capacity is the spare velocity cut at a published station, not the flood’s peak. Refuse path: a weir or a [Gulpgates](GULPGATES.md) vault that passes the ordinary pulse with every module Dark. If that path cannot, the bank is not a site. Object name on a pass: bubble cloud or vortex, not Gap Bridge.

**F2-T card.** One instrumented line, after F1-A. Water class logged, including one river-class grab. Spacing and burst period must meet the refresh rule or the block is a row of sparks. Meter: mean speed at $5s$ and $15s$ downstream against a no-array control, weir discharge during the run, and reactive species at the outlet. Co-verification: camera object, speed traverse, chemistry. A cut that dies by $15s$ demotes the article to a point disruptor. Do not sell a belt. A fish path through the line is a failed siting.

**Article W — Hydroweaver (confine along a line).** Horizon until F2-T has closed.  
Two facing lines, or one line and a return, firing on a published lattice. Warp strands hold a slow cadence. Weft strands cross them on the burst clock. The weave is the time-averaged mesh of short bridges, in rating water, or of bubble chains, on the wake path. It is rewoven every cycle. It inherits the Stormcrashers porosity doctrine: open area of the time-averaged mesh starts in $\phi \approx 0.30\text{–}0.50$ unless the flume says otherwise. A plugged weave is a solid sail and a failed article. It confines by slowing and turning a slice, the way a porous screen does. It does not hold a head. Ambient openness for a fish path is part of the rating, as on a Stormcrashers pylon grid. Object name on a pass: mesh, logged as bridge-mesh or wake-mesh. A curtain aimed at a hull or a person is the refused story, not a missed keep-out.

**F2-W card.** After F2-T. Meter: transmission $K_t$ across the curtain, reflection, refused volume on the weir, and keep-out leakage against the F0 floor. People stay off the curtain. A curtain that only works by arcing into the keep-out is an export failure. A curtain with no weir is not this article.

**Article P — positioned deck (walkable path).** Horizon until F1 has closed.  
The walkable surface is a buoyant deck: pads or a mat that already floats the published load with the array Dark. In rating water, short Gap Bridges in the $10\text{–}20\,\mathrm{mm}$ band sit in the joints and hold alignment. In river water the joint is a mechanical latch or a wake-path burst. It is not a Gap Bridge, and it is not rated from one. A passive tether, rated for the deck and one person, is the refuse path. If the joints drop, the deck stays a deck and the tether keeps it from leaving the line.

**F2-P card.** After F1, in a lined basin, and only after a Dark float test has carried the load. Meter: deck offset under the published load, joint object name during that offset, and tether load when the array is forced Dark. A person crosses only after that log. No one walks on a filament. A path with no passive tether is not this article. A river joint sold as a $15\,\mathrm{mm}$ bridge is not this article.

**What would make them likely, and what would kill them.**  
Likely, if the flume shows a repeatable wake from a burst, a refresh inside the rule above, and a weir that never needs the array. Killed, if the only stable object remains a $15\,\mathrm{mm}$ bridge in deionized water, or if natural-water conductivity collapses the field before a wake forms. F2 exists to choose. Until it closes, these articles are designs, not ratings.

Scale-out is the Regenerative Lattice Core count: more lines, more pads, separate isolate cards. Do not fuse T, W, and P into one river switch. Dark on any of them does not open a gulp, a jet, or a Stiff window.

## Energy, Materials, and Use

**Energy.** Pulsed duty is the article. Continuous high-power is refused for F1. Mid-array orientation draw $0.2\text{–}2\,\mathrm{kW}$ average. A single-module Gap Bridge cell may sit near $0.1\text{–}0.5\,\mathrm{kW}$. The thermoelectric bus is a return path, not a power plant.

A burst line is a different plant. At $20\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $50\,\mathrm{J}$ a shot, one module averages about $1\,\mathrm{kW}$. A $20$-module Article T line is on the order of $20\,\mathrm{kW}$ average before losses, and the supply must be specified before F2-T is called a campaign rather than a sketch. Do not price that line from the F0 cell.

**Materials.** Commercial laser, pulser, and a waterproof frame. Acoustic elements and coils may be fitted dark. Thermoelectric modules are ordinary bismuth telluride. No proprietary crystal. No licensed core cassette. Module A of the Regenerative Lattice Core is not an emitter.

**Uses that fit the file.** The seven implementations above, inside their campaign and their meter. Articles T, W, and P only after the campaign card that names them, and only as a slice, a porous curtain, or a positioned deck.

**Uses that do not fit.**

- A public fountain people can touch
- A sole flood defense, a seawall, or a filament used as a floor
- A seawater Gap Bridge
- A curtain or a strand aimed at a person or a hull
- Any mode whose success metric is harm to a person or a hull
