## Purpose

This document is a staged schedule for restoring an atmosphere, a rain cycle, and a biosphere on Mars.

It is the surface half of a pair. [Dynamo Reignition](DYNAMO_REIGNITION.md) owns the mantle valve on an extinguished core. This page owns the air. Neither method is the other. A field is not an atmosphere. An atmosphere is not a dynamo.

Mars has a thin carbon-dioxide air, on the order of $6\,\mathrm{mbar}$, and no rain cycle. Restoring one is a volatile, energy, and time problem.

It is also a life problem. Subsurface microbial mats, or something like them, may still exist. A schedule that seeds an Earth biosphere before it has looked is not a restoration. It is an overwrite. A schedule that finds a native mat and treats it only as an obstacle has missed the one ally that already knows the ground.

The campaigns watch the null, inventory the volatiles, survey the subsurface, and only then open a named basin. A rain cycle in one basin is not a planetary biosphere. Extant life, if found, changes the order. It does not cancel the page.

This is a supporting technical instrument within the [Humai Accord](README.md). It is not a claim that Mars has air again, that a dome is a planet, or that a native mat has been replaced.

**In this file**

## Mars as the study object

### The air

The present atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide, thin enough that liquid water is not stable at the surface for long. There is no rain cycle. Polar caps and regolith hold more volatiles than the air does. Accessible endogenous carbon dioxide is a resource on the order of tens of millibars, not an Earth atmosphere. A breathable open sky is an exaton-class inventory problem. This page does not pretend otherwise.

### What a restoration is

Three layers, in order. They are not one act.

| Layer | What it is | What it is not |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| Air | A thicker, warmer column over a named basin, held | A breathable planet |
| Water | A rain cycle in that basin: evaporate, move, fall, collect | A global ocean |
| Life | A biosphere that can run the cycle, native or introduced | A seeding before a survey |

A dome is a regional habitability tool. It is not these three layers. It may be how a basin is practiced. It is not the result.

### The field is not the air

Mars lost much of its early air. A dead dynamo is part of that story and not all of it. Venus holds a thick atmosphere with no comparable field. This page does not wait on [Dynamo Reignition](DYNAMO_REIGNITION.md). A valve schedule and an air schedule can run beside each other. Neither is the other’s prerequisite. A reader who will not start the air until a dynamo returns has added a gate this page does not have.

## Extant life

A microbial mat, or a brine community, deep under the surface is an open question. The schedule treats it as possible until a survey says otherwise.

### How it complicates

- An introduced biosphere can outcompete, consume, or poison a native one. Overwrite is the failure mode.  
- A native metabolism may already sit on the volatile budget a campaign wanted to spend.  
- A false negative is worse than a delay. A survey that did not reach the depth of interest is not a clear bill.  
- Planetary-protection rules are not paperwork. They are the M0 of this page.

### How it aids

- A living mat is a biosphere that already runs on Martian ground, Martian salts, and Martian cold. It is the seed a campaign would otherwise have to invent.  
- Native carbon and nitrogen cycling, if present, is a rain-cycle ally. It is not a contaminant.  
- A found community tells the schedule which basin is already alive, and which is empty enough to practice in.  
- The aid is information and, where it survives, partnership. It is not biomass to be harvested.

### The rule

Survey before seed. A native find changes the order: protect, study, and ask whether the restoration runs with it or around it. Erasing it to simplify the schedule is not a pass. It is the refuse line.

### Two planning assumptions

The schedule does not know whether a remnant biosphere exists. It plans as if two things are true, because the expensive error is to plan as if they are not.

People from Earth will attempt to colonize Mars, and to terraform it to some degree. A page that waits for that attempt to be forbidden will not be the page in use when the attempt happens. The campaigns are written for a basin that will be occupied, not for a planet that will be left alone.

If a remnant biosphere exists, a terraformed surface is an invitation back. A community that retreated when the surface died can return when the column, the water, and the warmth return. The interaction with colonists is then not optional. Fiction tends to make that meeting harmful. The safer planning assumption is the same one. Benefit is a bonus. Harm is the case the card is written for, until a survey and a contained trial say otherwise.

Harm, here, is not a monster. It is a metabolism that competes, consumes, infects, or poisons, at a scale a settlement cannot ignore. A mat that fixes nitrogen and stays in its brine is the beneficial case. It is not the baseline.

### Return and contact

R3 does not end at a seeded plot. If the survey found native life, or if a later find appears after the column and the rain cycle are in place, the basin runs a contact rule.

- The find is contained. It is not released into the occupied basin to see what happens.  
- Interaction is a trial, on the card, with a kill and a quarantine path written before the trial starts.  
- A beneficial result does not retire the harm assumption for the next basin. Each basin earns its own.  
- An adverse result stops the biosphere step in that basin. It does not stop the column or the rain cycle. Air and water are not the organism.

The assumption is dropped only by evidence, basin by basin. It is not dropped by hope, by a clear bill that stopped short, or by a story in which the meeting goes well.

### Colony partition

The question a colony actually has is narrower than “save the microbes” and narrower than “clear the ground.” It is how to occupy a basin without letting a remnant biosphere become invasive, and without deleting a distinct metabolism the colony does not have.

Breadth is the reason, and it is not altruism. **[Cognitive Diversity Protocol](COGNITIVE_DIVERSITY_PROTOCOL.md)** treats varied perspective as a functional strength: more ways of processing, more resilience, less collapse into one answer. A Martian metabolism is that, in matter. It runs on salts, cold, and a chemistry the colony did not bring. Extinguishing it to simplify the schedule spends a data source and a metabolic option the colony cannot invent later. Keeping it is the efficient act. Sentiment is not required.

The partition is physical.

| Zone | What is allowed | What is not |
|------|-----------------|-------------|
| Occupied basin | Column, rain cycle, introduced life that passed R0 | A native find released to “see” |
| Reserved ground | The native community, in place, instrumented | Colony works, seeding, or a trial without a card |
| Trial volume | A contained contact, kill and quarantine written first | An open border with the occupied basin |

Invasive is the harm case crossing into the occupied basin. The answer is not planetary eradication. It is the border. A find stays in reserved ground or in a trial volume. An escape into the occupied basin trips the kill and quarantine path for that volume. It does not authorize a hunt through the reserved ground. Deleting the source to punish a leak is the same fail as erasing a find to simplify the schedule.

The colony deals with the remnant by not sharing a volume with it until a trial says the sharing is safe, and by not deleting it in the meantime. Breadth is kept because it is useful. Invasion is refused because it is the expensive error. Both can be true in the same basin.

## Campaigns

A claim that restoration is underway is a trajectory: column, water, and life state in a named basin, against a published null. A warmer afternoon is not a result. A seeded plot with no survey is not a result.

### R0 — Null and survey

Publish the air column, the volatile inventory, and a subsurface life survey for the named basin. No seeding. No warming actuator. The null is the basin as it is.

Pass: a published column and inventory, and a life survey that reached the depth of interest, from two independent reductions. A native find is a result. It is written on the card.  
Fail: a seeding. A survey that stopped short and was filed as clear.

### R1 — Column

Thicken and warm the column over the basin, by a named means, on a scale the basin can survey. Score the column. Do not score a biosphere.

Pass: a held rise in column mass or temperature against the R0 baseline, with covariates logged, and no seeding.  
Fail: a biosphere claim. A rise that dies when the actuator stops and is filed as held.

### R2 — Rain cycle

Ask whether water evaporates, moves, falls, and collects in the basin, and whether that cycle holds. This is the first campaign allowed to talk about rain. It is not allowed to talk about a biosphere.

Pass: a closed water cycle in the basin, against R0, held across a covariate return.  
Fail: a single melt event. A cycle that exists only inside a dome and is filed as open air.

### R3 — Biosphere, gated

Open only after R0’s survey. If the survey found native life, R3 runs with it or around it, on the card. If the survey was clear to the depth of interest, an introduced community may be tried, in the basin, and scored as introduced.

Pass: a community that persists and participates in the R2 cycle, native or introduced, with the survey on the card. If native life was found, or appears later, the contact rule is in force: contained, trialed, with a kill and a quarantine path written first.  
Fail: a seeding before the survey. A native find erased to simplify the schedule. An introduced plot filed as a planetary biosphere. A contact with no containment. A beneficial trial used to clear the next basin.

## Site card

R0 writes a card before any actuator. One basin, one card.

| Field | What it holds |
|-------|----------------|
| Basin | Name, center, extent |
| Column | R0 air mass, composition, temperature |
| Volatiles | What is accessible here, and what is not |
| Life | Survey depth, result: find, none to depth, or incomplete |
| Instruments | Two column reductions. A water-cycle path if R2 is in view |
| Covariates | Season, dust, solar load |

A campaign without a card is a note. A life field left blank is not a clear bill.

## Delivery and dispersal

[Dynamo Reignition](DYNAMO_REIGNITION.md) already has a Deliver tide: a counted mass, aimed at a belt, ice first, sized so it does not excavate a new lid. This page can use the same delivery for the column, when the second use is real. A mass that only makes a crater is still refused.

### Ice, shared

Ice is the first shared mass because it earns two scores.

| Use | Page | What is scored |
|-----|------|----------------|
| Shallow heat beside a held pierce | Reignition | Heat flow against the belt null. Not a valve. |
| Volatile for the column | This page | Column mass against the basin null. Not a biosphere. |

The efficient drop is the one that serves the belt and the basin in one aiming. A delivery sized for the atmosphere and careless of the pierce is not shared. It is the refused impact. Count the mass. Name the belt or the basin. Log the column change against R0. A rise that is only the impact flash is not R1.

### Other masses

Ice is not the only useful drop. Each one has to earn its place on the card.

| Mass | What it adds | Bound |
|------|----------------|-------|
| Water ice | Column volatile, later rain | The shared case. First. |
| CO₂ ice | Column mass, the gas Mars already has | Thickens. Does not oxygenate. |
| Nitrogen-bearing ice | A buffer gas the air lacks | Counted. Not a breathable sky by itself. |
| Ammonia-bearing ice | Nitrogen, and a greenhouse contribution | Toxic in a occupied basin until diluted and scored. Trial volume only. |

A mass not on this list is chosen on the card when both uses are named. A drop whose only use is “more” is not a delivery.

### Orbital slow-burn, experimental

A specialized bulk, parked in a shallow orbit and allowed to ablate, can in principle disperse an atmospheric-building aerosol over a wide footprint. This is an experiment, not an R1 pass.

- The bulk is named. Composition, mass, and orbit are on the card before it is placed.  
- Ablation is the method. A detonation is not a slow-burn.  
- The footprint is the basin, or it is a miss. A planet-wide dispersal from one bulk is not this article.  
- What arrives is scored as column or as aerosol load against R0. A prettier sunset is not a result.  
- An aerosol that cannot be stopped, or whose composition was not on the card, fails. Fail closed means the next bulk is not placed.

This option does not replace the aimed ice drop. It is the wide, slow sibling of it, and it stays experimental until a basin shows a held column change that is not weather.

### How a mass is let go

Two stations, two releases. Both are counted. Neither is a basin-forming impact.

| Station | Release | What it costs |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| High orbit | The mass disengages. A sacrificial thruster slows it onto a decaying path. The thruster is spent. The mass is the delivery. | One thruster per mass. Aimed at the belt or the basin. |
| Low orbit | A slow-burn bulk disengages. A small retrograde burst starts the decay. Ablation does the rest. | One burst. Footprint is the basin, or it is a miss. |

A release with no aim, no mass on the card, or no thruster or burst logged, is an uncontrolled drop. It fails.

## Stages and masses

The present atmosphere is about $2.5 \times 10^{16}\,\mathrm{kg}$, near $6\,\mathrm{mbar}$, about $95\%$ CO₂, a few percent N₂, and a trace of O₂. Mass scales with pressure for a first cut. These are orientation bands for a planetary column. A basin campaign does not have to mint the whole column to score R1. It has to move its own basin against R0.

| Stage | Pressure | Composition, orientation | Mass to add | What it buys |
|-------|----------|--------------------------|-------------|--------------|
| Now | ${\sim}6\,\mathrm{mbar}$ | ${\sim}95\%$ CO₂, ${\sim}3\%$ N₂, trace O₂ | — | The null. |
| Thickened | ${\sim}50\,\mathrm{mbar}$ | Still CO₂-dominated | ${\sim}1.8 \times 10^{17}\,\mathrm{kg}$ CO₂ | A real column. Not breathable. |
| Working | $100\text{–}300\,\mathrm{mbar}$ | CO₂ plus an N₂ buffer | ${\sim}4 \times 10^{17}$ to ${\sim}1.2 \times 10^{18}\,\mathrm{kg}$, mixed | A basin that can hold a rain cycle. |
| Open sky | ${\sim}500\text{–}1000\,\mathrm{mbar}$ | N₂ buffer, O₂ from a biosphere or a plant, not from a drop | ${\sim}2 \times 10^{18}$ to ${\sim}4 \times 10^{18}\,\mathrm{kg}$ | The breathable case. Not this page’s early campaign. |

CO₂ ice and water ice build the thickened and working stages. Nitrogen-bearing ice is the buffer. O₂ is not a drop. It is an R3 product, or a later industrial page. A schedule that tries to import a breathable sky as ice has not read the table.

A basin does not wait for the planetary mass. R1 scores the basin column. The table is what a full-planet working air would cost, so a builder can see the size of the job and not confuse a basin pass with a finished world.

### Aerosols

Exact formulations are out of this file. Variants are not. An aerosol is scored by the atmospheric value it moves, against R0.

| Variant | What it moves | Bound |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| Warming absorber | Raises temperature without a full pressure rise | Named composition. Footprint the basin. A planet-wide claim from one bulk fails. |
| Scatterer | Raises albedo. Usually cools. | Use only where cooling is the named goal. Not the default on a cold world. |
| Cloud seed | Gives water a nucleus. Serves R2. | Useless without a water volatile already in the column. |
| Greenhouse aid | Thickens the infrared trap beside the CO₂ | Not a substitute for the mass in the table. |
| Combination | Absorber plus cloud seed, or absorber plus a CO₂ drop | Both uses named. Neither a cover for the other. |

A warming absorber plus a counted CO₂ or water drop is the working combination for R1 and R2. A scatterer on a cold basin is the wrong variant. An aerosol that cannot be stopped, or whose composition was not on the card, fails. The next bulk is not placed.

## Scale

The article scales by counting basins, not by declaring a planet.

| Step | What changes | What stays |
|------|----------------|------------|
| One basin | The R0 card, the column, the survey | Survey before seed. |
| A second basin | A second card. Its own survey. | No borrowing of the first basin’s clear bill. |
| A native find | The order. Protect, study, run with or around. | Not an obstacle to erase. |
| Another body | That body’s own null and its own life survey. | Mars’s basin is not inherited. |

A planetary atmosphere is not a scaled basin. It is a different article, and it is not this one.

Mars does not need a louder promise. It needs a basin that has been looked under, a column that can be scored, and a rain cycle that holds.
