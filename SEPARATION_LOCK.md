## Purpose

This document is the airlock sibling of [Mag-Path](MAG_PATH.md). It specifies one chamber that is the lock, the suit room, and the separator. A crew member dons and doffs here. Dust leaves the suit and the chamber here. There is no second cloister.

A multi-room airlock spends mass and energy on doors the first article does not need.

This **separation lock** does the whole flow in one volume, and it does not open the inner hatch until that volume has been cleansed and counted.

The chamber holds a captured-atmosphere sweep, pulsed coils, a light acoustic shed, a directed cleansing wash from a reservoir, a shallow floor pass, a perimeter drain ring, and a suit stand whose exterior faces a sealed side. It is not a second Mag-Path. It does not walk a crew member. It does not trap a boot. It does not fill itself with fluid.

Mars and the Moon do not share a cycle. Mars has a thin atmosphere to spend. The Moon does not. Neither cycle spends breathable air.

It serves Enterprise and Space Dock EVA locks. It is the lock [Mag-Path](MAG_PATH.md) already required and did not specify. It is not a second connector and not a suit design. It is an architecture and a first-article campaign. It is not a flight stamp.

## Short answers

**One chamber or two?**  
One. Don, passage, separation, and doff are the same volume.

**Does it spend cabin air to move dust?**  
No. Mars spends the thin local volume it sealed. The Moon has no sweep. Cabin air is recovered on the way out and used only to repress.

**What if the count fails?**  
The inner hatch stays shut. The crew are not trapped. The outer hatch remains the abort. A held count is a hold, not a loop and not an override.

**Does the Moon skip a stage?**  
Yes. The sweep. Everything from vacuum on is the same lock.

**Can a boot or a person be trapped?**  
No. Pulses end. Fluid drains. Power loss fails open. A full flood is refused.

**How many stands?**  
One rear-entry stand per suited crew member in that cycle. Two people and one stand is a queue, not a cycle.

**Do the grate steps finish the sole?**  
No. They open the patch. Boot wells at the stand finish the contact face. A hand wipe is not the cycle.

**What does the sniff do?**  
It classes the dust on the lock’s own surfaces against a published library. A hazard class holds the inner hatch. Unknown is a result, not a pass and not a hidden fail. It does not use the Mag-Path circuit. The wall film is a known signature. It is not dust.

## Two worlds

Martian air is mostly carbon dioxide, about $95$ percent, with a little nitrogen and argon. It is not a nitrogen sky. Surface pressure is about $0.6\,\mathrm{kPa}$, well under one percent of Earth. A sealed lock holds a thin volume of that gas. The volume is worth spending. It is not breathable air, and it is not thrown away into the cabin.

The Moon has no useful atmosphere. A lock there is already at vacuum. The Mars sweep has nothing to sweep with. The Moon cycle starts at the vacuum stage.

## One chamber

The lock is the suit room. Don and doff happen in it. Separation happens in it. A second room would be a second door, a second seal, and a second volume to clean. The first article refuses that.

Contamination control is the order, not an extra cloister.

- The suit is rear-entry. It stands against a sealed side of the chamber. The dirty exterior faces that side, or faces the outer hatch, and does not face the person once they have left it.
- Doff is through the back. The person steps into the chamber volume. The suit exterior stays on the stand. Handling the outside of a dirty suit in the breathing volume is a failed doff.
- Don is the reverse. The person enters a clean chamber from the cabin, steps into the suit, closes the back, and only then does the chamber depress.
- The chamber cleans the suit while the person is still in it, then cleans itself, then counts. The inner hatch is the only cabin path. It does not open on a failed count.
- After doff, a short chamber sweep or a hold at count confirms the volume did not pick up what the suit kept. Then the hatch.

Zero risk is the interlock plus the rear-entry stand. It is not a claim that no grain exists. A grain on a sealed stand, outside the breathing volume, is contained. A grain in the count is a held hatch.

## Order of operations

### Outbound

Chamber clean, at cabin pressure. Inner hatch open, then shut behind the crew.

1. Don on the stand. Back closed. Suit check.
2. Recover cabin air from the chamber into the cabin bus or a lock bottle. Do not dump it. A failed recovery aborts the depress. The crew stay at cabin pressure. They do not ride a vent.
3. Depress. Moon is already near vacuum. Mars vents or stores to local pressure. That gas is not breathable and is not the later sweep.
4. Outer hatch open. EVA.

### Return, Mars

Outer hatch shut. The lock holds the Martian volume it sealed. Crew still suited.

1. Mag-Path in the lock to idle.
2. **Sweep.** Pumps move only that captured volume. Flow is down and sideways, never one vector. Floor shear and wall shear drive loose grains to the perimeter.
3. **Trap, then return.** Each pass goes through a separator before the gas is used again. Two or three passes. An open swirl is refused.
4. Store or hand off the spent volume. It does not enter the cabin.
5. Pull the lock to vacuum.
6. **Magnetic train**, then a **light acoustic shed**.
7. **Sniff.** Low-intensity chirps on the lock’s own floor, walls, and drain cartridges. Match against the published library. Mag-Path in this chamber stays idle. The path circuit is not the sensor.
8. **Open the patch.** Two or three steps on the grate, or soles lifted on the stand. A grain clamped under a planted sole is not in the cycle until the sole leaves the deck. The steps loosen. They do not finish the sole.
9. **Boot wells.** Suited feet step into sealed wells at the stand. High-intensity magnetic and acoustic work in the well only. The sole is the target. The chamber is not. Then out. See Boot wells.
10. **Wash.** Directed nozzles, floor and wall, from the reservoir. Fluid runs and returns. The chamber is never full. Crew still suited. No port wets a visor. Fluid stays off the helmet face and below the suit’s critical seals.
11. **Floor pass.** A shallow band, $80\text{–}300\,\mathrm{mm}$, only on a sloped deck that can hold it, to take what the wash dropped. Then the perimeter ring. Recover to the reservoir. Dry the floor path.
12. Repress with recovered cabin air.
13. Count the volume, with the sniff result attached. Fail the count and the inner hatch stays shut. Repeat shed or hold. Do not open.
14. Doff through the back onto the stand. Suit exterior stays sealed to the stand.
15. Count again. Pass, then inner hatch.

### Return, Moon

No sweep. Seal, confirm vacuum, then steps 6 through 15.

A skipped train, standing fluid, a failed count, or a doff that exposes the suit exterior holds the inner hatch. It does not hold the crew.

## Abort and a held count

The outer hatch is the abort for the whole cycle.

- Mid-sweep, mid-wash, or mid-count, the crew can still leave by the outer hatch in the suits they are wearing. The lock does not have to finish to let them out.
- Power loss stops pumps and coils. Fluid drains. Nothing latches. IRIS may fail closed on a port. This lock fails open on a person.
- A count that fails is a hold. Repeat the shed or wait. Do not open the inner hatch. Do not invent a third room. Do not override a dirty count.
- A count that will not pass is still a hold. The outer hatch remains the way out. The cabin stays shut. Holding is not a failure of the crew and not a reason to average a bad count into a pass.
- After doff, if the second count fails, the person is already in the chamber and the suit is on the stand. They wait, or they don again and abort outward. They do not open inward.

## Sweep, and why it rotates only through a trap

One pass of thin gas moves loose dust and leaves the cling. A second pass helps only if the first pass removed what it carried.

- Directed shear, floor and wall, into the perimeter.
- Gas leaves through the separators, dust stays, cleaned gas returns.
- Two or three such passes. Then stop. Further passes on a dirty loop redisperse.
- No center swirl. No single jet. A jet re-entrains the pile it just made.

The sweep spends Martian atmosphere only. Cabin air is the repress, later, and it is not the working fluid.

## Drains

No single outlet. The ring is a set of independent drains, in the same spirit as a Gulpgates array: one clog is a remainder, not a lock failure.

- A ring of drains at the perimeter. Four is the minimum. Six is the planning number.
- Floor slopes out to the ring, including the corners. Corners are part of the ring, not dead pockets.
- Any one drain may clog. The others take the wash and the floor pass. Shared blockage of the ring is a shared remainder, published, not hidden by averaging a clear drain with a dead one.
- A lock with one crescendo in the middle is refused: one blockage is a failed lock, and a center drain fights the side vector.
- A side-only single drain is the same failure with a different address.
- Each drain seals on its own. Recovered fluid and solids do not enter cabin air. A sight on the ring, in the spirit of [Hydrofilaments](HYDROFILAMENTS.md), says whether a drain is passing. It does not add a new collector.

## The chamber

- **Volume.** The existing EVA lock. No second room on the first article.
- **Sweep ports.** Low and at the wall, paired so flow has a down-component and a side-component.
- **Coils.** In the deck and in the wall panels, behind a dielectric face. No open conductor. Local drivers. A failed coil isolates.
- **Acoustic shed.** Light emitters in the vacuum stage only. A tap, not a cabin tone. Aimed at fabric and at soles.
- **Reservoir.** One recyclable cleansing fluid. Filtered and reused. Not cabin water. Not the Mag-Path glycol. Isolated from cabin air.
- **Wash.** Directed nozzles or sheet ports. Floor and wall vectors, as the gas sweep. Fluid returns as it runs. The volume is never filled. A full flood is refused: mass, entrapment, and, in low- $g$, a blob instead of a fill line.
- **Floor pass.** Shallow, $80\text{–}300\,\mathrm{mm}$, only where the deck can hold it. Then the ring.
- **Deck.** A grate over a shallow pit. The pit feeds the drain ring. Two or three steps, or soles lifted on the stand, before the wash. A closed contact patch is not a cleaned sole.
- **Wall.** Structural wall is metal or composite. The dust never sees it. The face the grit meets is a renewable gel film. See Wall film.
- **Stands.** Rear-entry. The suit’s outside faces the stand seal or the outer side. After doff it is not in the breathing volume.
- **Count.** Particle count before the inner hatch, and again after doff. Two passes. One fail holds the hatch.

A boot may be a Mag-Path shell. The lock pulse is not a Mag-Path draw. The two do not share a driver. During the lock cycle, Mag-Path lots in that lock are idle.

## What still clings

The sweep takes loose grains. The vacuum stage takes the rest.

| Dust | How the lock takes it |
|------|----------------------|
| Loose, any composition | Mars sweep, floor and wall, into the ring. Moon has no sweep |
| Ferromagnetic and strongly paramagnetic | Pulse train, after vacuum |
| Charged fines | Fast edge on the pulse, plus the short charge-relax before the train |
| Packed or wedged | Light acoustic shed in vacuum, then the directed wash and the floor pass |

The charge-relax step is brief, low, and shielded. It is not an open electrode and not a shock. It does not run on a bare crew.

What the lock does not promise: a clean suit from the sweep alone, or collection of a grain that no stage touched. M0 measures the fraction after the full cycle. The article does not invent it. The cleansing fluid and the wall-film mix are named by M0-L. They are not invented here.

The wash is the last mechanical pass. It runs suited. It clumps what the pulse and the tone dropped, and it returns to the reservoir as it goes. The floor pass takes the rest. Neither fills the chamber.

## Resonance sniff

The lock uses the same idea as Mag-Path’s surface circuit, on its own surfaces. It does not use the path.

After the shed, and before the wash, low-intensity chirps run in the floor, the wall panels, and the drain cartridges. Each chirp is a short sweep. The return is matched against a published library of particulate signatures: regolith classes, metal fines, ice, and the named suit-dust families the campaign has measured.

- **Low intensity.** Enough to read a signature. Not enough to re-entrain a pile or to be a cabin tone.
- **Library.** Published with the lock. A match names a class. No match is “unknown.” Unknown is a result. It is not a hidden fail and not a pass.
- **Hazard class.** A library entry marked hazard holds the inner hatch until the cycle is repeated or the crew abort outward. It does not open inward.
- **Not a lab.** The sniff classes the dust the cycle just handled. It does not assay the crew, the suit interior, or the cabin.
- **Not the path.** Mag-Path is idle in this chamber. Chirps stay off the Mag-Path acoustic window. A path tone and a lock chirp are not the same circuit.
- **No trail.** Signatures are cycle results, attached to that count. They are not a movement log and not a wearer score.

The sniff tells the count what is still in the room. The count still decides the hatch. The wall film is in the library as itself. A reading of the film is not a dust class.

## Boot wells

A few steps on the grate open the patch. They do not clear a sole. A hand wipe is the inefficient option: it puts the contact face in the breathing volume and spends a person on a job the lock should do.

The close is a well, not a second room.

- **At the stand.** Two wells, one per foot. The suited boot steps in. The suit stays on. No one handles the sole.
- **Sealed.** The well mouth closes on the boot collar, or the boot seats into a boot-shaped throat. Dust that leaves the sole stays in the well. It does not return to the chamber.
- **Aggressive, local.** High-intensity magnetic pulses and a short acoustic burst, inside the well only. Stronger than the chamber train. Aimed at the contact face and the tread. Not at the visor. Not at the chamber.
- **Out the bottom.** The well floor is a grate over the same pit. Grains fall. They do not pile under the next boot.
- **Not a trap.** Power loss opens the mouth and kills the pulse. A foot lifts out. Fail open, as the path does.
- **Then the wash.** Wells first, so the chamber wash is not fighting a loaded sole.

Hand cleaning remains a maintenance act on a stand that has already failed its well. It is not the cycle.

The shell helps before the well. Ablative panels on the boot, specified in [Mag-Path](MAG_PATH.md), run a low repulsion and a metered micro-film during EVA. They cut the load. They do not finish the sole. No hot-swap pad. The well is still the closer. The $0.2\,\mathrm{mm}$ check is after the well. Panel film is not the wall film and not the path glycol.

## Wall film

Sharp particulate abrades a bare wall. A harder liner only delays the wear and then adds its own fines. The lock does not ask the structure to survive that.

The dust face is a gel film. The wall behind it is the structure.

- **Metered, not a seep.** The film is fed from a bladder or a porous liner at a published rate. It does not weep from cracks. An unplanned seep is a failed wall, isolated, not a mode.
- **Sacrificial.** The grit cuts the film. The film leaves with the wash and the floor pass. The structure does not.
- **Restored.** Each cycle, or continuously at a low rate, the liner puts the film back. That is the regeneration. It is a reservoir and a pump, not a living skin.
- **Fluidic.** Soft enough that a grain embeds or slides instead of chiseling the wall. Viscous enough that it stays on a vertical face in the local gravity, and that in zero- $g$ it stays a film rather than a blob. Thickness is a campaign number. Orientation: a few millimetres, replaced, not grown without limit.
- **Known to the sniff.** The film’s signature is in the library. A match to the film is “film,” not regolith and not unknown.
- **Recovered.** Worn film goes to the reservoir with the wash, is filtered, and is reused or discarded as fines. It does not enter cabin air. It is not the Mag-Path glycol.
- **Off the crew.** The film is on the walls and the deck face. It is not sprayed onto suits. A film on a visor or a seal is a failed feed.

A slime from the cracks would be the right instinct and the wrong control. The same material, metered, is the efficient option: the wall that wears is the wall you meant to replace.

## Wash law

Circulation, not immersion.

- One fluid, one reservoir, filtered between cycles.
- Directed wash covers suits, stands, and walls. It does not fill the lock.
- In zero- $g$ or on the Moon, there is no fill line. The wash is spray or sheet only. The floor pass is used only on a deck that can hold a band.
- A port that wets a visor, or a fault that fills the chamber, is a failed wash. Power loss stops the pumps. Fluid drains. Nothing holds the crew in a tank.
- Reservoir mass is the planning load. A lock-volume of fluid is refused.

## Cycle

The order is the section above. This is the interlock.

- Mars: sweep and trap before vacuum. Moon: no sweep.
- Vacuum before the magnetic train and the acoustic shed.
- Wash only after the shed. Drain and dry before repress.
- A full chamber of fluid is a failed state, not a mode.
- Repress with cabin air only after the ring has cleared.
- Inner hatch only after that.

A skipped train, standing fluid, or an unsealed drain holds the hatch. Straps and ordinary lock procedure still hold the crew. The lock is not a restraint and not a tank.

## Frequencies

Orientation. M0 replaces them.

- **Pulse.** Bipolar or unipolar bursts, fundamental in **$1\text{–}10\,\mathrm{Hz}$** for the train, each burst a faster edge so $dB/dt$ does the charged-grain work. Slow enough that a boot is not held. Fast enough that a grain leaves fabric.
- **Edge.** The edge, not the fundamental, is the collector. M0 sets the edge that moves charged fines without heating the suit or the coils past the published budget.
- **Duty.** Low. The train is seconds, not a soak. Coils reject their own heat to the lock structure. They do not feed the Mag-Path liquid loop.
- **Harmonics.** Kept off Mag-Path’s grip band ($40\text{–}120\,\mathrm{Hz}$) and off its acoustic window. The lock and the path are not in one ear.

No audible cabin tone. The train may be felt as a tap. It is not a voice channel.

## Force and the boot

The lock must not do Mag-Path’s job and must not undo it.

- Between pulses, force on a planted Mag-Path sole is inside Mag-Path’s free band, $\le 8\,\mathrm{N}$.
- During a pulse, a sole may see a brief tug. It stays under Mag-Path’s walk ceiling, $40\,\mathrm{N}$, and it ends with the pulse. A deliberate step succeeds.
- Gloves and tools are free. A pulse that holds a glove is a failed pulse.
- Power loss: coils off. Nothing in the lock latches. IRIS may fail latched. This lock fails open.

## Scale

The first article fits a crew suit. The chamber is meant to grow.

Later frames — assistive exoskeletons, mobile suits for mining and hauling, machines that need large lift and torque — use the same lock at a larger volume. The cycle does not change. Sweep, vacuum, shed, sniff, wash, floor pass, drain, count, doff, count. Rear-entry stands scale with the frame. One stand per frame in that cycle. Drains stay a ring. The wash stays directed. The volume is never filled.

A larger lock is not a new article and not a second connector. It is this chamber with more room. Force and torque stay in the suit. The lock does not supply them and does not restrain them. Fail-open, the outer-hatch abort, and the two counts hold at any size.

## Later tunnel

When a station is built on site, and launch mass is no longer the limit, a length of tunnel may replace the box.

The tunnel is a later article. It is not the first lock. It is this cycle stretched into a walk: a grate floor, a pit under it, emitters and the sniff in the walls, two ends. Walking works the sole. Gaps drop the grains. The length is also a buffer. A mining face, or any section that may need to be cut off, sits at the far end. Delicate modules sit at the near end. Damage isolates down the tunnel. It does not sit against the habitat.

The first article does not spin the crew and does not launch a rotor. A short grate drum inside a later tunnel is allowed if a flat grate leaves a film. The room itself does not rotate. Vestibular load is not a lock mode.

Same rules at any length. Directed wash. Never a full flood. Two counts. Outer end is the abort. Inner end waits. Fail open.

## Closing

Separation Lock is one chamber, not a chain of rooms.

Dust leaves the suit while the crew is still in it, on local air if Mars has it, then under vacuum, then under a directed wash that returns to its reservoir. The chamber is never filled. The suit stays on a rear-entry stand when the person leaves it. The chamber counts twice. The inner hatch is the only cabin door, and it waits on the count. What a stage cannot take is a published fraction, not a footnote. The cabin stays the cabin.
