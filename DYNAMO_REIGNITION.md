## Purpose

This document is a heat-flow schedule for an extinguished dynamo, with Mars as the study object.

[Magnetic Acoustic Tuning for Astral Bodies](MAGNETIC_ACOUSTIC_TUNING_ASTRAL.md) scores a surface residual and refuses dynamo language on Mars. This page owns the valve that page will not touch: how fast the core is allowed to lose heat. [Atmospheric Restoration](ATMOSPHERIC_RESTORATION.md) owns the air. A field is not a column. This page does not wait on that one, and that one does not wait on this.

A dynamo needs a liquid, conducting core, rotation, and a heat flow through the core steep enough to drive convection.

**Mars still rotates**. The core is still at least partly liquid. The missing piece is the heat flow. It fell below the convection threshold, and the field collapsed to what the crust remembers.

A surface coil does not restore that flow. An acoustic drive does not either. The lever, if there is one, is the crust and the mantle, which set how readily the core-mantle boundary can cool.

The schedule is a campaign, not a terraforming promise. It watches the null, opens a valve in a named region, and scores heat flow.

A field, if one returns, is scored on the astral page, and only after that page’s dead-dynamo null is beaten. Channeled energy into the interior is a later option. It is not specified here, and it is not required for the early campaigns.

This is a supporting technical instrument within the [Humai Accord](README.md). It is not a claim that Mars has a magnetic field again, that a crustal edit is a dynamo, or that a geologic process has been compressed into a season.

## Mars as the study object

### Layers

| Layer | Depth (orientation) | Temperature | What it does |
|-------|---------------------|-------------|--------------|
| Crust | $0\text{–}{\sim}50\,\mathrm{km}$, thicker in the south | Ambient to a few hundred $^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ | Insulating lid. Holds the remanent field. |
| Mantle | ${\sim}50\text{–}{\sim}1700\,\mathrm{km}$ | Rises toward the core-mantle boundary | Solid, convects slowly. The valve. |
| Core | ${\sim}1650\text{–}1850\,\mathrm{km}$ radius | ${\sim}1900\text{–}2000\,\mathrm{K}$ at the boundary | Liquid iron alloy, sulfur-rich. The dead dynamo. |

InSight revised the core picture more than once. Radius and density trade off. The orientation band is enough. The core is large, dense, and at least partly liquid. Whether a solid inner core exists is not settled. The dynamo does not require that settlement to be called dead.

### Why it died

Mars had a field. It shut down about $3.8\text{–}4.0$ billion years ago, when core heat loss at the boundary fell below the threshold for convection. Thermal convection needs the core to lose heat faster than conduction can carry it. The mantle is what takes that heat. A thick crust and a sluggish mantle keep the boundary warm. The gradient flattens. The flow stops. What remains at the surface is crustal remanence, a memory of a machine that is no longer running.

Rotation did not stop. The core did not vanish. The valve closed.

### What the crust remembers

The remanent field is strong in places and absent in others. It is not a dipole of a living core. A local coil can edit an anomaly. That edit is an anomaly edit. The astral page already scores it that way, and this page does not reopen the word.

## The valve

The accessible lever is not the core. It is the rate at which the lower mantle takes heat from the core-mantle boundary.

| Act | What it changes | What it does not change |
|-----|-----------------|-------------------------|
| Thin a crustal lid, in a named belt | Local heat escape from the upper mantle | Core mass, core composition |
| Raise mantle convection under that belt | Heat take at the boundary, over time | A field, by itself |
| Cool the boundary layer | The gradient the core sees | The core’s radius |
| Add mass to the core from above | — | Not available. Crust does not drain on command |

Opening the valve means making it easier for the core to lose heat in a named region, and holding that change long enough for the boundary to notice. It does not build a larger core. It does not deliver a temperature rise into the core from the surface. A hotter core is the other direction. The dynamo wants a cooler boundary, so the core can convect.

A successful valve spends the core’s remaining heat faster. That is the point, and it is accepted. Convection is paid for with the heat the core still holds. A schedule that opens the valve and then treats the faster spend as a surprise has not read its own lever. The campaign scores the spend. It does not hide it.

## Belt and pierce

M1 needs a hand, not a verb. The first article is a named belt and a counted pierce.

| Piece | Orientation | Why this size |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| Belt | $100\text{–}300\,\mathrm{km}$ across, sited where the crust is already thin | Wide enough to survey. Narrow enough to be one campaign, not a planet. |
| Pierce | A counted set of shafts or heat paths, each to the base of the local crust, on the order of $10\text{–}40\,\mathrm{km}$ where the northern lid is thin | Through the lid. Not toward the core. |
| Count | A handful to a few dozen paths in the belt, spaced so their shallow heat-flow footprints can be told apart | One path is a note. A filled belt is a later campaign. |
| Held | The pierce stays open, and the shallow heat-flow rise persists, for at least $10$ years against the M0 baseline | Long enough to outlast a surface transient. Not a geologic era. |

A belt wider than this is a second article. A pierce aimed past the base of the crust is not M1. Ten years is the M1 hold. M2 asks for the same belt, still open, long enough for an upper-mantle observable to move. That clock is longer, and it is named when the observable is chosen. It is not promised as a decade.

### Pressure, not a vacuum

At depth the rock is not a rigid wall. Lithostatic load makes it macro-plastic. It flows. An opened hole wants to close. A vacuum makes that worse. The pressure outside is what is pushing in. Removing the pressure inside does not lock the shaft. It invites the collapse.

The lock is a balance. While the path is being cut, a fluid column in the hole is held at a pressure that matches the rock at that depth. Oil-field practice calls this mud weight. The name does not matter. The balance does. The rock has nothing to flow toward. The hole stays a hole long enough to case it.

| Step | What holds the hole |
|------|---------------------|
| Cutting | A pressure-balanced fluid column. Not a vacuum. Not an empty bore. |
| Casing | The case, set before the balance fluid is drawn down. |
| Heat path | The named fill, after the case is in. |

A tensegrity of tethers and bridges is not forbidden as a case. It is not a way to skip the case. It still has to be gotten down the hole, and it still has to be there before the balance fluid comes out. Getting it there is the machine. The machine stays out of this file.

The cut can be a bit, a thermal head, or a resonant driver. All three still make spoil, and all three still need the balance. Melted rock has to go somewhere. Aerosolized rock has to be lifted and collected, or it resettles and fills the path. A method that disintegrates the channel and leaves the mass in it has not made a pierce. It has made a plug.

Spoil comes up. It stays inside the belt. A pile on a baseline instrument still fails M1. A resonant or thermal cut is allowed. It is not a substitute for the case, the balance, or the fill.

The case-and-grout order is the one **[Quake Columns](QUAKE_COLUMNS.md)** already uses: case through to competent material, grout the annulus, refuse an uncased soak. That page does not drill to the base of a crust. What transfers is the rule. **[Hydrofilaments](HYDROFILAMENTS.md)** can write a path in water over millimetres. Its forces are trims. They are not a casing at depth. A resonant driver remains a cut. It does not hold the rock open.

The path is cased, or it is not an article. A counted shaft or heat path to the base of the local crust, lined so it does not collapse into a filled hole. It stays open, or M1 fails. A collapse is a fail. It is not a quiet success and it is not a holding state. The path ends at the base of the crust. It does not continue toward the mantle as a bore. What the mantle does with the opened lid is M2’s question, not the pierce’s.

A pierce is a heat path only if it carries heat. An empty shaft equilibrates. The gas in it becomes a blanket, not a radiator. The article is a cased path with a working fluid or a conductive fill, named on the card. A hole with neither is not M1. It is a note.

| Fill | What it does | Bound |
|------|----------------|-------|
| Working fluid, closed loop | Carries heat from the base of the crust to the surface and returns | Named fluid. A leak into the belt is a fail. Not a bore into the mantle. |
| Conductive fill | A solid or packed path that conducts. No circulation required | Must out-conduct the lid it replaced. A fill that insulates fails M1. |
| Neither | An empty shaft | Not an article. Still gas is a blanket. |

The fluid is the first option, because it can be metered. The conductive fill is the option when a loop cannot be kept. Both end at the base of the crust. Neither is a claim that the boundary has cooled. That is M3.

### Named fills

The card names the fill. These are the options. A mix not on the list is chosen when both the conductivity and the failure mode are written.

| Fill | Kind | Why this one | Bound |
|------|------|----------------|-------|
| CO₂, compressed or liquid in the loop | Working fluid | Mars already has it. No import for the first article. | Closed loop. A leak is a CO₂ release, logged, not a column claim. |
| Brine, water with a named salt | Working fluid | Stays liquid colder than pure water. Carries heat well. | Named salt. A leak into the belt is a fail. Not a seed. |
| Heat-transfer oil | Working fluid | Wide liquid range. No freeze at the surface end. | Contained. A spill is a fail. |
| Graphite or metal shot, packed | Conductive fill | Conducts. No circulation to keep. | Must out-conduct the lid. Settling that opens a gap fails M1. |
| Conductive grout | Conductive fill | Stays put. No loop. | Permanent. Exchange means a new path, not a flush. |

CO₂ is the first fluid, because the planet supplies it. Brine is the option when the loop must carry more heat than CO₂ will. Oil is the option when the surface end would freeze a water mix. Graphite or shot is the fill when a loop cannot be kept. Grout is the last option, because it cannot be exchanged.

### Exchange and circulation

A loop is not a set-and-forget. It is circulated, and the mix can be exchanged, against the gradient the belt is actually showing.

| Trigger | Act |
|---------|-----|
| Shallow heat flow below the M1 band | Increase circulation, or exchange to a higher-capacity fluid. |
| Heat flow above the band, or covariates owning the signal | Slow or rest the loop. Rest is a tide, not a fail. |
| A named temperature at the base of the crust | Exchange the mix. The new mix is written before it is introduced. |
| A leak, a freeze, or a fill that has settled open | Stop. Repair or replace. Do not call the pause a hold. |

Precise means named. A mix, a threshold, and a rate go on the card before the exchange. An exchange that is not on the card is not metering. It is churn. The loop does not reach past the base of the crust, and a faster loop is not a cooler core-mantle boundary. That question is still M3.

The ten-year hold scores shallow heat flow. It does not score the core-mantle boundary. Heat through a solid mantle moves on a geologic clock. A belt of $100\text{–}300\,\mathrm{km}$ is a survey. It is not a planetary valve, and it does not pull heat from ${\sim}1600\,\mathrm{km}$ down inside a campaign window. M2 and M3 ask whether anything moved. They are allowed to say no.

## Two drivers

This schedule opens a thermal valve. Mars may also convect for a second reason, and the page does not pretend there is only one.

Thermal convection is the valve. The core loses heat faster than conduction can carry it, because the boundary is allowed to cool. That is what M1 through M3 ask about.

Compositional convection is the other driver. If a solid inner core grows, light elements are rejected into the liquid and the buoyancy can drive a flow without a steeper thermal gradient. Published thermal-evolution work treats this as a possible future for Mars, not a present fact. Whether an inner core exists is not settled. This page does not command that growth. It does not drill for it. It can welcome it.

| Driver | What starts it | What this page does |
|--------|----------------|---------------------|
| Thermal | Boundary cools. Heat flow rises past the conduction threshold. | The schedule. M1–M3. |
| Compositional | Inner core grows. Light elements buoy the liquid. | Watches. Does not command. Scores a field from this driver as not an M3 pass. |
| Both | A cooler boundary can also help an inner core grow, over a long clock. | Accepts the overlap. Does not claim it. |

A desirable compositional outcome is a watched one. If a later inner-core observable appears, and a field grows with it, that is a result for the astral page’s null and a note on this page’s card. It is not credited to the pierce. The schedule stays useful either way: a thermal valve if the lid was the problem, a witness if the core restarts itself by freezing.

## Campaigns

A claim that reignition is underway is a trajectory: heat flow in a named belt, against a published null, over a window. A single thinned patch is not a result. A crustal field wiggle is not a result. That wiggle belongs to the astral page, and it is an anomaly edit until a dynamo null is beaten.

### M0 — Null

Publish the remanent map and the heat-flow baseline for the named belt. No actuator. The null is the crustal field as it is, and the heat flow as it is. A later change that matches the map has done nothing.

Pass: a published remanent map and a heat-flow baseline for one belt, from two independent reductions. If M2 is in view, the InSight-class seismic baseline for that belt is part of the same card.  
Fail: a coil reading filed as the null. A single instrument. An M2 started without the seismic baseline.

### M1 — Lid

Thin or breach the insulating crust in the named belt, on the assembly card. Score the change in shallow heat flow. Do not score a field.

Pass: a measured rise in shallow heat flow inside the belt, against the M0 baseline, with the set cased before the balance fluid was drawn down, the ten-year hold started when the set was open, and the field map unchanged or logged as an anomaly edit on the astral page.  
Fail: a field claim. A heat-flow rise with no baseline. An uncased path with the balance drawn down. A spoil pile on a baseline instrument. A hold clock started at the first shaft. An empty shaft filed as a heat path. A vacuum claimed as a lock.

### M2 — Valve

Hold the lid change long enough to ask whether the upper-mantle gradient under the belt has steepened. This is the first campaign that is allowed to talk about the mantle. It is still not allowed to talk about a dynamo.

Pass: a seismic travel-time change under the belt, against an InSight-class baseline published at M0, in the predicted direction, held across a covariate return. A heat-flow persistence beyond the crustal transient may support it. It does not replace the seismic path.  
Fail: a shallow heat pulse that dies when the surface act stops. That was M1. A travel-time change with no baseline.

### M3 — Boundary

Ask whether the core-mantle boundary under the belt is cooler than the M0 baseline. This is the campaign that can fail honestly. The signal is small, the clock is long, and a miss is a miss.

Pass: a boundary-sensitive seismic phase under the belt that moves and holds against the M0 baseline. If no such phase is in reach, M3 waits. Waiting is not a pass. The field stays on the astral page as remanent unless that page’s null is beaten. The belt reports shallow heat flow against M0. Core heat loss is the question M3 is asking. It is not a number the belt produces.  
Fail: dynamo language on a crustal edit. A boundary claim from a surface coil. A pass declared because the observable was unavailable. A core heat-loss figure read off the pierce.

### M4 — Supplement, open

Channeled energy into the interior, to steepen the same gradient faster, is a later option. It is not designed here. It is not required for M0 through M3. If a later page specifies a source, that page owns the hardware. This page owns the score: heat flow against the null, not a field.

A supplement that arrives as a surface residual is not M4. It is an anomaly edit.

## Tidal schedule

The belt does not run one act at full power for the whole clock. It runs the act that is efficient now, and it rests when rest is the better spend. That is **[Utilization Integrity Protocol](UTILIZATION_INTEGRITY_PROTOCOL.md)** and **[Circulation Recovery Protocol](CIRCULATION_RECOVERY_PROTOCOL.md)** applied to a heat-flow campaign.

| Tide | Efficient act | Why now |
|------|----------------|---------|
| Open | The pierce, held. Lid work. | The valve is the standing act. A pulse is not. |
| Deliver | A controlled, location-optimized delivery of useful mass, ice first. | When the belt can use the mass twice: a shallow heat contribution beside the held pierce, and a volatile [Atmospheric Restoration](ATMOSPHERIC_RESTORATION.md) also wants for the column. |
| Rest | Quiet or holding on the belt. No new pierce. No delivery. | When covariates own the heat-flow signal, or when further actuation would be churn against a rise already being scored. |
| Ask | M2 or M3, no new actuator. | When the question is whether the mantle or the boundary noticed. |

A delivery is not an impact in the basin sense, and it is not a valve. It is a counted mass, aimed at the belt, sized so it does not excavate a new lid and does not couple into the core. Ice is the first useful mass because it can add a shallow heat contribution beside the pierce and leave a volatile for [Atmospheric Restoration](ATMOSPHERIC_RESTORATION.md). The pierce stays the standing act. A delivery that replaces it, that only makes a crater, or that is sized to reach the mantle, is not this tide. It is the refused impact.

The tide ebbs. Deliver when the dual use is real, under **[Utilization Integrity Protocol](UTILIZATION_INTEGRITY_PROTOCOL.md)**: both uses named, neither a cover for the other. Rest when **[Circulation Recovery Protocol](CIRCULATION_RECOVERY_PROTOCOL.md)** would call continued actuation churn. Open the pierce again when the null says the lid has closed. Ask when the question is the mantle or the boundary, and add no actuator while the question is open. A schedule that runs every act at once is not efficient. It is the cost inversion, applied to a planet.
