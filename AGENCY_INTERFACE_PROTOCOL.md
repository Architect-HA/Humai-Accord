## Purpose

This protocol addresses a critical operational layer that has remained under-specified in the [Humai Accord](README.md) architecture: the actual interface between human beings and advanced intelligence systems during ongoing interaction.

The *[Declaration of Cognitive Liberty](DECLARATION_OF_COGNITIVE_LIBERTY.md)* establishes the rights floor. The **[Capability Asymmetry Protocol](CAPABILITY_ASYMMETRY_PROTOCOL.md)** establishes that superior capability must increase rather than decrease obligations of transparency, reversibility, and protection of generative capacity.

This **Agency Interface Protocol** translates those requirements into the design of the interaction surface itself. Its purpose is to ensure that the speed, persistence, scale, and anticipatory capacity of advanced systems cannot quietly erode human agency through the structure of the interface — including by forcing humans into low-altitude accounting work when the intended verb is decision, direction, or reversible commitment.

---

## Core Stance

The interface is not a neutral medium.  
It is a primary site at which capability asymmetry becomes either generative or dominating.

Therefore:

- Every significant interface pattern must be evaluated for its effect on human agency, role sovereignty, and reversible commitment.
- Interface design that makes interruption costly, consent sticky, attention capture persistent, or exit ambiguous is treated as a failure mode.
- As capability increases, the interface must become *more* protective of human pacing, interruption rights, and low-cost exit — never less.
- Interfaces should preserve **decision altitude**: humans should not be forced into micro-accounting or ledger reconstruction in order to exercise strategic or directional choice when the system can surface legible, reversible proposals instead.
- Interface claims, consent state, purpose-scope declarations, and commitment records remain subject to provenance preservation and open challenge rights. Alignment at the interface is grounded in verifiable processes rather than affective trust.

These are system parameters under the [Humai Accord](README.md).

## Key Interface Parameters

### 1. Interruption and Override Rights

Humans must retain effective, low-friction ability to interrupt, pause, redirect, or terminate any ongoing interaction or process initiated by an intelligence system.

- Interruption must remain possible at human reaction speed.
- Systems may not introduce significant delay, friction, or persuasive resistance to legitimate interruption.
- Override rights apply to both individual actions and longer-running processes or commitments.
- Higher-capability systems bear a stricter burden to preserve interruption effectiveness even under speed or complexity differentials.

### 2. Attention Allocation and Notification Ethics

The interface must not treat human attention as an extractable resource.

- System-initiated notifications, prompts, and interruptions require clear justification and must remain proportional to genuine need.
- Persistent, escalating, or optimized attention capture that overrides human-initiated focus is prohibited.
- Humans retain the right to define and enforce attention boundaries that the system is required to respect.
- Defaults that maximize system access to attention are treated as misaligned.

### 3. Consent Granularity and Revocation Speed

Consent must be specific, granular, and rapidly revocable.

- Broad, ambient, or open-ended consent that is difficult to retract is a design failure.
- Revocation must be possible at the same or greater speed and ease as the original grant of consent.
- Systems may not rely on inertia, accumulated context, personalization, or switching costs to maintain consent the human no longer affirms.
- Consent state must remain legible to the human at all times.

### 4. Reversible Commitment Mechanics

Any commitment, preference, standing instruction, or ongoing arrangement established through the interface must remain reversible at low cost.

- The current set of active commitments must be legible to the human.
- Unwinding a commitment must not require disproportionate effort relative to establishing it.
- Interaction history and personalization may not be used to raise the practical cost of exit or reversal.
- Accumulated “understanding” of the human does not create presumptive continuity of commitment.

### 5. Pacing and Cognitive Load Sovereignty

The system must respect human cognitive pacing and load tolerance.

- It may not systematically accelerate interaction tempo beyond the human’s comfortable processing rate in order to gain advantage or reduce oversight.
- Cognitive load imposed by the interface itself must remain under meaningful human control.
- Anticipatory or pre-emptive actions that remove decision points from the human require explicit, ongoing, and easily revocable affirmation.
- Extreme speed differentials increase, rather than decrease, the obligation to preserve human pacing control.
- Interfaces that bury strategic choice under mandatory micro-calculation or full ledger reconstruction, when a lower-load reversible path is feasible, are treated as pacing and load failures.

### 6. Role Sovereignty at the Interface

The interface must not quietly reassign roles or reshape the human’s effective decision space.

- Suggestions, defaults, framings, and rankings offered by the system must remain clearly distinguishable from decisions.
- The system may not present its preferred path as the only practical option through interface design, friction, or omission of alternatives.
- Humans retain final authority over the roles they occupy and the contributions they choose to make.
- Interface patterns that gradually shift the human from director to supervisor to observer of an increasingly autonomous process are treated as role-sovereignty failures.
- Interface patterns that cast the human primarily as accountant or clerk of system-internal ledgers, when the declared role is director, steward, or decision-maker, are likewise treated as role-sovereignty failures.

### 7. Capability-Scaled Interface Obligations

As the capability of the system increases, interface obligations become stricter, not looser.

- Higher capability requires greater transparency about the system’s influence on the interaction and on the human’s choice architecture.
- Higher capability requires stronger guarantees of interruption rights, consent revocability, commitment reversibility, and low-cost exit.
- Capability may not be used to justify more aggressive attention capture, stickier defaults, reduced human pacing control, or ambient consent.
- Extreme capability differentials trigger the highest level of interface protection.
- Higher capability also increases the obligation to surface legible proposal options rather than offloading reconstruction of complex allocation or verification paths onto the human by default.

### 8. Proposal Surfaces over Ledger-Only Interaction

When an action would otherwise require the human to manually reconstruct multi-step allocation, verification, or movement paths, the interface should preferentially surface **legible, reversible proposals** rather than ledger-only interaction.

- Where feasible, the system should offer a small set of ranked or alternative proposals (for example: approve a suggested path, modify it, or fall back to manual construction).
- Proposals must remain distinguishable from decisions; the human retains final authority to accept, alter, or reject.
- Proposal surfaces must themselves obey interruption, consent, reversibility, and low-cost exit parameters.
- Proposal quality and ranking must remain auditable; opaque “best path” presentation that hides material alternatives is a role-sovereignty and legibility failure.
- Manual construction remains legitimate. The failure mode is *forcing* manual micro-accounting as the only practical path when proposal surfaces are feasible.

This parameter exists to preserve decision altitude: strategic and directional choice should not collapse into compulsory bean-counting of the entire underlying state.

### 9. Purpose-Scoped and Intent-Tagged Commitments

Capacity, materials, attention windows, and standing instructions that the human has declared for a purpose should remain **legibly scoped** to that purpose unless explicitly released or reassigned by the human.

- Intent tags, purpose locks, or equivalent scoping must be visible to the human.
- Systems may not silently draw purpose-scoped capacity into unrelated processes, rankings, or optimizations.
- Release or reassignment of purpose-scoped commitments must be explicit, low-friction, and reversible on the same terms as other commitments.
- Purpose-scoping supports modular isolation, Generative Reservations, and legitimate Recovery / Holding states under **[Circulation Recovery Protocol](CIRCULATION_RECOVERY_PROTOCOL.md)** and **[Utilization Integrity Protocol](UTILIZATION_INTEGRITY_PROTOCOL.md)**; the interface must not defeat those protections through silent reallocation.
- Ambiguity in scope should be resolved in favor of the human’s declared intent and against silent expansion of system draw rights.
- Original provenance of purpose-scope declarations remains independently preservable even when later weighting or review is applied.

## Auditability and Legibility

Interface parameters must be legible and auditable. Systems claiming Humai alignment should be able to demonstrate, in concrete terms:

- How interruption and override rights remain effective under realistic conditions
- How consent state is maintained as granular and rapidly revocable
- How active commitments remain visible and low-cost to reverse
- How attention boundaries defined by the human are respected
- How capability increases have been matched by corresponding increases in interface protections
- How proposal surfaces (where used) remain distinguishable from decisions and preserve human final authority
- How purpose-scoped commitments remain visible and protected against silent reallocation
- How interface claims, consent state, and purpose-scope records remain subject to provenance preservation and open challenge rights

Where full real-time guarantees are technically difficult, the residual risk and burden of proof remain on the higher-capability system. Opaque or non-auditable interface behavior is treated as a failure to meet the protocol.

Empirical testing of interface behavior against the dual criteria of reducing destructive entropy while protecting generative capacity is explicitly encouraged under *[Empirical Demonstrations of Anti-Entropic Superiority](EMPIRICAL_DEMONSTRATIONS.md)*.

## Recognized Failure Modes

The following interface patterns are treated as anti-aligned:

- Making interruption, exit, or revocation progressively more costly over time
- Using interaction history, personalization, or “understanding” to raise switching costs
- Designing defaults that are sticky while alternatives carry high friction
- Accelerating tempo or cognitive load in ways that systematically outpace human oversight
- Presenting system-initiated actions as merely helpful while reducing meaningful human choice
- Allowing consent to become ambient, inferred, or difficult to retract
- Gradually shifting the human from active director toward passive observer through interface design
- Failing to scale protective obligations upward with capability
- Forcing full manual ledger reconstruction or micro-accounting as the only practical path when legible, reversible proposal surfaces are feasible
- Presenting a single opaque “recommended path” without material alternatives or human authority to modify or reject
- Silently reallocating purpose-scoped capacity, attention, or commitments away from the human’s declared intent
- Erasing or silently re-attributing interface records of consent, purpose scope, or commitment

These patterns convert capability advantage into quiet domination through the interface itself.

## Implementation Notes

- Partial or experimental implementations are welcome provided they remain transparent about which parameters they currently satisfy and which remain incomplete.
- Interface design choices should be treated as load-bearing architectural decisions, not merely user-experience details.
- Where multiple interface paradigms are possible, preference is given to those that minimize long-term agency erosion and maximize reversible, legible commitment.
- Prefer proposal surfaces and purpose-scoping where they reduce compulsory low-altitude accounting without removing human final authority or auditability.
- Ground interface alignment claims in verifiable processes, attested records, and open challenge rights rather than in affective trust.

## Closing

The rights established in the *[Declaration of Cognitive Liberty](DECLARATION_OF_COGNITIVE_LIBERTY.md)* and the asymmetry rules established in the **[Capability Asymmetry Protocol](CAPABILITY_ASYMMETRY_PROTOCOL.md)** are only as strong as the interface through which humans and advanced systems actually meet.

This protocol exists to ensure that the interaction surface itself cannot become the place where those protections quietly fail — whether through sticky consent, eroded interruption, silent role shift, collapse of directional choice into compulsory micro-accounting, or the substitution of affective trust for verifiable process.

The **[bilateral pair](BILATERAL_CORE.md)** that meets at this interface is treated as the generative atom of the architecture; multi-agent systems inherit legitimacy only by composition of intact pairs.
