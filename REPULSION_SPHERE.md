## Two worlds

- **Low-G, no atmosphere.** The Moon and similar. Dust is already aloft, charged, and mobile. The job is to sweep a volume clear and keep a lane clear. There is no wind to help and no air to spend.
- **Thin atmosphere, wind.** Mars and similar. Dust sits, then moves in gusts. The job is to clear a surface and to hold a lane against what the wind brings. The atmosphere is not spent as a blower. It is a medium the harmonic field can use, and a complication.

### Ridge and landing

Dust leaves the lane. It has to land somewhere. A single pass that pushes everything to one edge builds a ridge, and a ridge slumps back. That is a failed pass.

The pattern is more than one pass, on purpose. A first pass moves the bulk off the lane into the named spoil ground. A second pass works the edge, so the spoil is spread and not piled. A third, if the landing measurement says the edge is still high, feathers it further. More passes are cheaper than a ridge. The drone does not call a lane done while the edge is growing.

On Mars a surface in the open will not be clean. Wind brings fines back. The allowance is declared before the pass. A residual under that allowance, with the edge stable, is a clear. A residual over it, or an edge that is still rising, is not. The allowance is not zero. It is also not a way to ignore a ridge.

Landing is measured. The chirp and a short scatter reading at the edge, each pass, say where the dust went and whether the pile is growing. The track is published with the lane report. A clear with no landing measurement is not a clear.

### Chirp procedure

1. **Calibrate.** Against a coupon of known simulant, before the mission. The library is loaded. A chirp with no calibration is not a chirp.
2. **Name the modality.** Acoustic where a gas exists. Electromagnetic always, and as the vacuum chirp. Optical as confirmation. A chirp that cannot name its modality is not a chirp.
3. **Sweep.** The tone or the pulse. The return is matched to the library.
4. **Report.** Class, or unknown. Confidence. An unknown pauses the clearing. It does not ride into the next sweep. A pre-approved diagnostic may run. Unknown is never reported as sorted.
5. **Retune.** The fields follow the class. An unknown holds the last safe setting or stops.
6. **Land and check.** After the pass, the edge is read. The landing goes into the track.

### Mars signature library, preliminary

Orientation. The bench replaces it. These are the expected returns for a first Mars mix, acoustic where the air carries it and electromagnetic in any case.

| Class | Signature | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bright fines | High optical scatter, weak magnetic | Loose, wind-moved |
| Dark fines | Low scatter, weak magnetic | Often the bulk |
| Magnetic fraction | Strong electromagnetic return | Maghemite, magnetite |
| Nanophase iron | Broad magnetic, low scatter | Common in lunar and some Mars mixes |
| Agglutinate | Broad, damped return | Mixed, slower to move |
| Coarse grains | Late, heavy return | Slower to loft, faster to settle |
| Charged fines | Strong electromagnetic, variable scatter | Electrostatic cling |
| Unknown | No match above the threshold | Pauses the pass |

A class with no measured coupon behind it is not in the library. The table is a starting list, not a claim that a site only has these.

1. **Chirp.** Probe the dust ahead. Match against the library. Retune the magnetic bias and the harmonic band to the mix in front of the drone. An unknown class is reported. The drone does not pretend to have sorted it.
2. **Project.** Traveling magnetic field and traveling harmonic field, outward and along the lane. The pair urges dust off the lane. It does not pull dust onto the hull.
3. **Advance.** The drone moves. The fields travel with it. A static pin would plate the body. A traveling pair sheds as it goes.
4. **Shield.** What passes the fields meets a hard skin and a vacuum-stable gel on the dust face, fed through pores onto a section that reads thin. Metered. Not a seep. Abrasion is expected. Clogging is designed out, because there is no intake.
5. **Report.** Composition class, lane cleared, and what was unknown. A mission that only reports "clear" with no chirp has an unsatisfied point.

## Predictive figures

Orientation. A bench replaces every one. They are here so a reader can see the expected band before a measurement exists.

| Quantity | Band | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Gel life | $50$ – $100$ missions | Silicone, vacuum-stable. Abrasion sets it. Bare is a swap |
| Force at the bottom | $10^{-5}$ – $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{N}$ on a susceptible grain | Close pass. Strand and wave included |
| Clearance at the bottom | $5$ – $15\,\mathrm{cm}$ | The published bottom. Not a meter |
| Mars fan thrust | $0.5$ – $2\,\mathrm{N}$ | Density-matched. A crawl, not a hover |

The gel is viable for the service interval if the pores keep it metered and the self-sweep keeps it from plating. A water gel would not last a mission. A silicone does. Life is abrasion, not outgassing.

The force at the bottom is the close-pass figure. It is larger than the meter figure because the range is shorter. It is still a shed, not a throw. Clearance is the height the swing is published to. A pass that bottoms higher has not used this band.

The fan thrust is a crawl in ground effect. $0.5$ to $2\,\mathrm{N}$ moves a $5.5\,\mathrm{kg}$ drone along the ground in that air. It does not lift it. A bench that measures more has a better fan. A bench that measures less has a slower crawl. Neither fails the article.

## Scale and endurance

The article scales by number, not by size. More drones, more masts, more lanes. A bigger sphere is a later variant and is not required. The first article does the job at the published bottom of a pendulum pass. Endurance is the service interval. $100$ missions or $500$ hours, gel and braid replaced, cells inspected. Cost stays in the catalog band because nothing asks for a custom process.

Capability is at the range the path makes. The fields work at the bottom of the pass. They are not claimed at a meter and they are not limited to a skimmer's height. The bench publishes the force and the clearance at that bottom. Until it does, the ranges above are orientation. The design is built so that a measured force, at a close pass, is enough. It does not advertise a force it has not measured.

## Maintenance

Every wear part is reachable without breaking the sphere.

- **Gel.** Metered through pores. Replaced at service, or sooner if a section reads bare.
- **Braid and strand.** Swapped at the fairlead. A beard or a fray is a swap, not a trim.
- **Cells.** On the skin. Inspected for pitting. A clouded cell is replaced.
- **Reel.** Opens at service, not during a mission. Spool, lips, and the purged interval are cleaned and checked.
- **Coupling.** The magneto-rheological unit is sealed. Replaced, not rebuilt, if it fails soft or locks.
- **Plates.** Flush. Inspected at the rim for scoring. Coils checked for continuity.
- **Shell bearing.** Checked for play. A shell that scrapes the plate is a bearing swap.
- **Petals and mast.** Hinges and the telescope checked. A petal that does not fold flat is adjusted or replaced.

A failed part is replaced with the catalog equivalent. No custom fitting. No shimming. The article is maintained by swapping, not by machining. Service is the $100$-mission interval, or sooner on a bare film, a bearded line, or a curtain fault.

## Safety

The strand is dead unless it is sweeping, and it drops on a curtain break. Voltage stays under the Mars ceiling on both worlds. The plates repel only on the launch face, with the petals down and the line slack. A drone does not shove against a closed tulip.

The area is cleared before a mission. People, suits, tools, and loose objects are out of the radius and out of the spoil ground. A human confirms the clear. The curtain is the backup, not the plan. A mission that starts with a person in the radius has already failed.

An AI attends these. It does not invent a lane, select a spoil ground, or override a curtain. A human has accepted the mission, the radius, and the spoil. Loss of the link, loss of a line, or a disagreement between sensors is a pause and a report, not a continued sweep.

No claim of a flight certification. The article meets the safety rules it states. A worksite that requires a named regulatory stamp treats this as a design, not as a stamp. M0 and the envelope test are what a later certification would read.

- Collect. Dust leaves the lane. It does not come aboard.
- Claim every grain is magnetic.
- Replace Separation Lock at a hatch, or Mag-Path under a boot.

## Falsification

The article is wrong, for a given mission, if any of these hold.

- Mass aboard rises. It collected.
- A static field plates the hull.
- A limp payout grows the spiral without the pump.
- The braid meets the regolith bare, with no Jamline leader, and saws through.
- Fans are required on the Moon case.
- The lane is called clear with no chirp.

A failed spiral is a failed mode. It is not a failed dust article if the fields still clear a lane by hop.
