## Purpose

This document is a dust article for places *[Separation Lock](SEPARATION_LOCK.md)* does not reach.

The lock clears a suit at a hatch. A pad, a lane, a panel field, and a worksite stay dirty. Vacuuming clogs. Blowing spreads.

This page moves the dust with traveling fields and does not take it aboard.

The drone **projects a traveling magnetic field and a traveling vibrational harmonic field**. It chirps, matches the return against a published library, and retunes. It is remote-controlled or attended by an AI under a declared mission. It is not a collector.

## Short answers

**Does it collect dust?** No. Dust leaves the lane. Mass aboard does not rise.

**Does it fly on the Moon's magnetic field, on static, or on dust recoil?** No. All three are refuses.

**What moves it?** Reaction wheels point it. On the Moon, a hop and a tether. On Mars, fans. Solar on the skin. No fuel tank. No wing.

**Why a fishing line?** A bulky tether abrades and collects. A braid in fishing-line diameter does not become a net.

## Two worlds

- **Low-G, no atmosphere.** The Moon and similar. Dust is already aloft, charged, and mobile. The job is to sweep a volume clear and keep a lane clear. There is no wind to help and no air to spend.
- **Thin atmosphere, wind.** Mars and similar. Dust sits, then moves in gusts. The job is to clear a surface and to hold a lane against what the wind brings. The atmosphere is not spent as a blower. It is a medium the harmonic field can use, and a complication.

The two are not averaged. A published mission names which world it is in. Moon-like and Mars-like scale to other sites of the same kind. An airless low-G body uses the Moon article. A thin, dusty atmosphere uses the Mars article. A new site does not get a third variant until it is neither.

## Flight patterns

- **Moon and airless.** A pumped spiral around an anchor is the efficient pattern. One cast, a growing ring, a reel home. A pad larger than the line gets a second anchor and a second spiral, with a hop between them. A grid without a tether spends hops for no gain.
- **Mars and thin air.** A racetrack over the lane, crosswind, so a pass does not fly back through what it just moved. Into the wind is the return leg, fans working, fields shedding. In a blow the drone flies the same lane as a kite on the tether. The frame does not change.

### Kite posture, Mars

A mode, not a variant. The tether is required above a published wind threshold, so a gust does not take the drone. Below that threshold, and while the wind looks to stay down, the drone may fly untethered on the fans. If the wind rises past the threshold and looks to hold, it returns to an anchor and reattaches. When the wind falls and looks to stay down, it may detach again. Attachment is per task. A radius mission stays on the line. A calm racetrack may come off it.

More than one anchor may be set around a base. One drone can cover more than one zone by reeling home, hopping or flying to the next piton, and casting again. A zone without a drone is not uncovered if an anchor is waiting.

The magnetic field is not a control. Mars has no global field worth the name. The kite is the line, the bridle, and the wind.
- **Both.** Overlap the passes. A single line through a lane leaves a ridge of what was urged aside. A second pass, offset, is the clear.

## Fans, Mars article

The fan is a translation tool in thin air. It is not a vacuum and it is not a filter.

- **Shutter.** The outlet vent is shut except while the fan is blowing, then shut again. Open time is the blow. A vent left open is an intake the article refused.
- **No filter tray.** A screen that extends, spins, and shakes is a collector with extra steps. It clogs, it adds a mechanism, and it contradicts the no-intake rule. Refused.
- **How it stays clear.** The same traveling pair that clears a lane is biased across the fan face while the shutter is shut, so fines do not sit on it. The blades are a shedding geometry, pitched to throw a grain outward rather than hold it. Bearings are sealed.
- **Size.** Catalog ducted fans, sized for a density-matched flow, not for an Earth hover. Published diameter and thrust on the Mars-like bench. A fan that only works in sea-level air has failed the article.

## Core premise

Move the dust. Do not catch it.

A magnetic field alone misses the grains that are not magnetic. An acoustic field alone is weak where there is no air. The pair covers more of a real dust than either one. The chirp says which mix is in front of the drone, and the tuning follows. What gets past the fields meets a shielded skin, not a filter.

*[Separation Lock](SEPARATION_LOCK.md)* already refuses the claim that every grain is ferromagnetic, and already classes dust by a chirp against a library. *[Mag-Path](MAG_PATH.md)* already uses a low field and a light acoustic tick to keep fines off a face. This page is those two tools, traveling, and pointed outward.

## Definitions

- **Sphere.** The drone. A shielded body, field emitters around it, no intake.
- **Traveling magnetic field.** A field that moves relative to the body, so dust is urged along a direction rather than pinned to the hull.
- **Traveling harmonic field.** A vibrational wave, acoustic where a medium exists and a coupled vibration of the field hardware where it does not. It sheds and sorts. It does not blow.
- **Chirp.** A low-intensity probe. The return is matched to a published library of dust signatures. Unknown is a result. It is not a pass.
- **Lane.** The volume or surface the mission is clearing. The dust goes somewhere that is not the lane. It is not collected.
- **Tether.** A fishing-line-scale braided line on a reel. The Moon translation mode for a radius around an anchor. Not a power cable. Not a data line.

## Propulsion

No tank. No wing. Solar on the skin runs the fields, the chirp, the wheels, and the reel.

- **Attitude.** Reaction wheels, both worlds. They point the drone. They do not translate it.
- **Moon and airless.** A grip-and-hop for a short move, Mag-Path's hold-and-release on a short leg. A tether and reel for a pumped spiral around an anchor. Momentum goes into the anchor. Fans are absent.
- **Mars and thin air.** Fans for translation. The hop as backup when the air will not push. The tether still available for a spiral around a lander.
- **Power.** Cells on the body, not on a deployable. The drone does not slew to find the sun. The same fields that clear a lane keep the cells clear. A small battery holds a reel-home and a lights-out chirp. It does not size the mission.

### Where the dust goes

Off the lane, and not onto a neighbor. Stormcrashers — Mars already refuses dual-use steering of dust onto someone else's pad. This page inherits it. A published mission names the spoil ground. A lane cleared by burying the next pad has failed.

### Who points it

Remote or attended by an AI under a declared mission. Agency Interface holds. A human accepts the mission, the spoil ground, and the radius. An AI does not invent a lane. Exit from the mission is cheap. The drone reels home.

### What is refused as thrust

- The Moon's crustal field. Too weak, too patchy.
- A static charge around the body. Net force is zero.
- Recoil off the dust. Real, small, and gone when the lane is clear. A trim, not a mode.
- A wing or a deployable array.

## Build

Chosen for what a small shop can buy, not for a research alloy. Quality and safety set the floor. Cost breaks a tie.

- **Body.** Aluminum alloy shell, 6061 class, with a hard anodize. Light, cheap, known, and stiff enough for a small drone. A composite shell is a later mass save, not the first article.
- **Emitters.** Copper windings on a ferrite or laminated-iron core for the magnetic pair. A piezoelectric or small voice-coil driver for the harmonic pair. Both are catalog parts. Intensity published. A pin that plates the hull is a failed setting.
- **Cells.** Commercial space-rated or terrestrial high-efficiency cells on the skin, on a thin cover glass. No deployable. The fields keep them clear.
- **Film.** A small onboard reservoir, dispensed on command onto the dust face between passes, or when the chirp says the face is loading. A wipe edge or a small roller, catalog. Metered. A known volume, a published interval, refilled at service. Not a seep. It does not run during a blow, and it does not feed the tether, the cells, or the vent.
- **Braid.** Ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene, Spectra or Dyneema class, fishing-braid construction. Orientation diameter $0.2$ – $0.4\,\mathrm{mm}$. This is the cost-effective strong line. Wire rope at this job is heavier and collects more.
- **Leader.** Fluorocarbon-class for the general case. A fine stainless leader where a rock edge is expected. Short, so the cost of the better abrasion piece stays small.
- **Reel.** A sealed winch with a set drag, commercial small-drone class, and a swivel. Dust-lipped. Not open.
- **Leg.** Aluminum, same alloy, with a hold-and-release pad. Mag-Path's law. It does not cling.
- **Battery.** A small commercial pack for reel-home and a lights-out chirp only.

Numbers are orientation. M0 replaces them.

## The tether

A cast, a reel, and a braid. The fishing problem and this one are the same. A thin line that is strong for its diameter, a reel that pays out and pumps, and a short heavier section where the line meets the abrasive thing.

- **Braid.** Ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene, Spectra or Dyneema class, in a fishing braid diameter. Low stretch, high strength for the section, and small enough that it does not become a dust net. A bulky cable is refused. It abrades, it collects, and it is mass.
- **Leader.** A short abrasion section at the anchor end and at the drone end. Fluorocarbon-class or a fine metal leader. The braid is bad against a sharp edge. The leader is where the line meets the regolith and the piton.
- **Reel and drag.** A winch with a set drag. Pay out on the cast. Reel in a little at one point in the circle, pay out on the other, so the spiral grows. That pump is the energy. The anchor takes the momentum.
- **Swivel.** At the drone end, so the circle does not twist the braid into a knot.
- **Anchor.** A driven piton or a Mag-Path grip. Not a weight on dust.

A spiral out and a reel home is a mission inside the line's reach. A flight past the anchor is not this mode. The line is not a power tether and not a comms line. Jamline's posture applies. It holds under load and sheds when it should, rather than sawing.

## The method

1. **Chirp.** Probe the dust ahead. Match against the library. Retune the magnetic bias and the harmonic band to the mix in front of the drone. An unknown class is reported. The drone does not pretend to have sorted it.
2. **Project.** Traveling magnetic field and traveling harmonic field, outward and along the lane. The pair urges dust off the lane. It does not pull dust onto the hull.
3. **Advance.** The drone moves. The fields travel with it. A static pin would plate the body. A traveling pair sheds as it goes.
4. **Shield.** What passes the fields meets a hard skin and a renewable film on the dust face, fed from the onboard reservoir between passes. Metered. Not a seep. Abrasion is expected. Clogging is designed out, because there is no intake.
5. **Report.** Composition class, lane cleared, and what was unknown. A mission that only reports "clear" with no chirp has an unsatisfied point.

## Falsification

The article is wrong, for a given mission, if any of these hold.

- Mass aboard rises. It collected.
- A static field plates the hull.
- A limp payout grows the spiral without the pump.
- The braid meets the regolith bare and saws through.
- Fans are required on the Moon case.
- The lane is called clear with no chirp.

A failed spiral is a failed mode. It is not a failed dust article if the fields still clear a lane by hop.
