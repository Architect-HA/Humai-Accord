## Purpose

This document is a prior about aerostatic assembly. It asks whether a pressure-equilibrium layer in a dense atmosphere can do the job [Life Wells](LIFE_WELLS.md) assigns to a sump: hold a solvent, a gradient, and a carbon source long enough for chemistry to persist, without a rock and without a surface.

It treats the gas giants of this system, the decks where a terrestrial body has a thick sky, and the theoretical case of an extrasolar terrestrial world whose atmosphere is dense enough that a floating layer is a better cradle than the ground. Comets are carriers, not cradles.

It is not a detection. It is not a claim that a floater exists. It is the other half of the cradle question, filed separately so it does not blur the well.

---

## The deck

A sump holds reactants because rock confines them. A deck would have to hold them because the air does.

A pressure-equilibrium layer is a band where the density of the gas matches the density of a droplet, a particle, or a membrane, so the thing neither rises out nor falls through. On a gas giant that band is a fact of the atmosphere. On a thick-sky terrestrial world it is a possible fact. Nebula-like coalescence is the picture for that band: favorable gases and aerosols collect where buoyancy parks them, and concentrate if something stops them diluting.

Four requirements.

1. **Persistence.** The layer lasts longer than a droplet’s fall. A convective cell that overturns in minutes is not a deck.
2. **Confinement without rock.** A droplet, an aerosol coagulate, or a membrane has to keep reactants together. Open air dilutes. This is the requirement Life Wells uses to retire an aerosol, and it is the one this page has to meet or refuse.
3. **A gradient.** Redox, pH, or a photochemical product from above meeting a reduced gas from below. A uniform layer is a tank, not a reactor.
4. **Carbon, or a demonstrated substitute.** Methane, CO₂, or organic haze. A deck of hydrogen and helium alone is not a biochemistry we know.

If those four hold, a deck is a cradle candidate. If confinement fails, it is weather.

### What Earth shows

Earth has no aerostatic organism. Cloud droplets host bacteria that were lifted from the ground or the sea. Residence times are minutes to days, not seasons. The analogy is delivery and survival, not origin. A measured Earth cloud is a control: a deck that fails persistence and confinement, and is inhabited only by what fell into it or was thrown up.

### Why a thin sky still loses

Life Wells retires the aerosol while a surface is forming. This page does not reopen that. A thin sky, a Mars, a Mercury, has no equilibrium band a droplet can sit in. The question is asked only where the atmosphere is the body, or dense enough that the ground is the hostile face and the deck is the buffered one.

### Flip against the well

On a rocky body with a thin or forming sky, the well wins. On a body with no rock, or with a dense sky over a hostile ground, the deck is the only buffered layer available. The prior flips with the body, not inside one page.

## This system

### Jupiter

Hydrogen and helium. Clouds stack by condensation temperature. Ammonia ice near the top, cloud tops around $120\,\mathrm{K}$. An ammonium hydrosulfide deck near $200\,\mathrm{K}$. A water cloud is expected deeper, near a few bar. Galileo’s probe entered a $5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ hot spot and found a tenuous ammonia wisp near $0.5\,\mathrm{bar}$, a modest ammonium hydrosulfide structure from about $0.76$ to $1.34\,\mathrm{bar}$, and only a weak, uncertain water signal near $1.6\text{–}4.5\,\mathrm{bar}$. It reached about $22\,\mathrm{bar}$ and over $400\,\mathrm{K}$ without a massive water cloud. The entry was a downdraft. Water activity in the temperate range has been argued to be biologically permissive, unlike Venus. Droplet composition is the open limit.

The floater hypothesis is old and untested. The band can exist outside a hot spot. Confinement does not. Prior for the band: high. Prior for an ecology: speculative.

### Continuity

Sagan and Salpeter asked this question in 1976. In *Particles, Environments, and Possible Ecologies in the Jovian Atmosphere* they wrote that if hypothetical organisms could manage slow, powered locomotion and coalescence, they could grow large enough to achieve buoyancy, and that ecological niches for sinkers, floaters, and hunters appeared to exist in the Jovian atmosphere. They also warned that a plausible, internally consistent ecology would not demonstrate that life on Jupiter was likely.

This page keeps that warning and moves the question. They imagined organisms that could hold a level. The open requirement here is the one their niches assumed: confinement that beats dilution, for long enough, with a gradient and carbon. A floater is a picture. A deck that parks a chemistry is the claim. The picture is theirs. The four requirements, the Earth control, and the flip are the continuation.

### Saturn

The same stack, colder, with ammonia and a deeper water cloud. Storms and a hexagonal polar jet say the deck is dynamic, not a still pond in the sky. Same confinement problem. Prior: as Jupiter, slightly colder.

### Uranus and Neptune

Hydrogen, helium, and methane. Methane is about $1\text{–}4$ percent on Uranus and $2\text{–}5$ percent on Neptune. Voyager occultations put a methane cloud near $1.2\,\mathrm{bar}$ on Uranus and $1.6\,\mathrm{bar}$ on Neptune. Models give bases near $1.24$ and $1.64\,\mathrm{bar}$, droplets up to $100\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, and high settling, so the cloud precipitates rather than standing thick. H₂S appears more abundant than ammonia. A water–ammonia cloud is predicted at several hundred bar, below any temperate reachable layer. Prior: lower. The visible deck is methane ice and short-lived. The water is too deep.

### Venus

Surface about $460\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and $92\,\mathrm{bar}$, $96.5$ percent CO₂. Clouds from about $47$ to $70\,\mathrm{km}$. In the $50\text{–}60\,\mathrm{km}$ band, temperature passes through $0\text{–}75\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and pressure through about $0.5\text{–}1\,\mathrm{bar}$. Droplets are concentrated sulfuric acid, about $75\text{–}98$ percent by weight. Water activity in those droplets is $\le 0.004$, two orders of magnitude below the $0.585$ limit for known extremophiles. Phosphine has been reported and disputed. A cloud habitat would be aerostatic by necessity. The acid, the water activity, and the residence time are why this page does not rest on it. Prior: the most discussable terrestrial case, and the one published chemistry most clearly stresses.

### Titan

Nitrogen atmosphere, surface pressure about $1.5\,\mathrm{bar}$, surface temperature about $94\,\mathrm{K}$. A methane cycle with clouds, rain, and lakes. Tholins from photochemistry are a complex organic haze, a carbon source in the sky. A methane-droplet deck is a different solvent and is not merged with a water well. Prior for aerostatic methane life: speculative. The organics are real. The solvent is not water.

### Comets

Organics, ices, and a transient coma within a few AU of the Sun. Rosetta at 67P measured water, CO, CO₂, and organics, present while the nucleus is warm and gone when it is not. Residence is the apparition. A comet can seed a deck or a well. It is not a cradle. Prior for assembly in a coma: low.

### Passed over

Io, the Moon, Mercury, Mars, and the small dry moons have no deck that meets the four requirements. Mars has ice clouds and a $610\,\mathrm{Pa}$ sky. That is weather, not a well in the air. A gas giant’s deep metallic hydrogen is not a deck. Brown dwarfs are a colder, starless Jupiter case, and are not given a section until a temperate band is the point.

## The extrasolar case

A terrestrial world with a dense atmosphere is the case this page is for, beyond the solar system.

If the ground is hot, irradiated, or chemically hostile, and a pressure-equilibrium band in the sky is temperate and persistent, the prior flips relative to Life Wells. The deck becomes the buffered cradle and the surface becomes the face that fails. Favorable gases and aerosols collect in the band because buoyancy parks them. That is the nebula-like picture. It is a picture of concentration, not of a guaranteed chemistry.

What would have to be true, and is not yet measured for any such world:

- A layer stable against overturn on a biological timescale, not a convective minute
- A solvent in that layer, water or a named substitute
- A gradient across it
- Confinement that beats dilution, by droplet, coagulate, or membrane
- Carbon

Habitable-zone super-Earths and mini-Neptunes with thick hydrogen envelopes are the observational class. A transmission spectrum that shows a deck is not a spectrum that shows a cell. A world with a dense sky and no stable layer is not this article. A sampled sterile deck of the right temperature would lower the prior.

### Nearby cases

These are the nearest worlds a reader can point at. None is a detection. They are viability sketches.

- **K2-18 b.** About $38$ parsecs, in the habitable zone of an M dwarf, about $2.4$ Earth radii and $9$ Earth masses. JWST sees CH₄ and CO₂. A Hycean reading puts a thin hydrogen envelope over a water ocean. A mini-Neptune reading puts a thick gas envelope and no surface. The second is the deck case. The first is a well under a sky. Both are live. Neither is a cell.
- **LHS 1140 b.** Within $40$ parsecs, habitable zone, lower instellation than K2-18 b, and a non-zero chance of a rocky surface under a thick sky. A deck is possible if the envelope is heavy. A surface is possible if it is not. Unsettled, and closer to a test than most.
- **TRAPPIST-1 e.** About $12$ parsecs, Earth-sized, in the zone. JWST allows a heavy atmosphere or a bare rock. If the atmosphere is dense, a deck becomes discussable. If it is absent, the world is Life Wells or nothing.
- **TOI-700 d.** About $31$ parsecs, Earth-sized, in the conservative habitable zone. Likely rocky. A deck requires an atmosphere it may or may not have kept. A sketch, not a sounding.
- **Proxima Centauri b.** The nearest, about $1.3$ parsecs, minimum mass near Earth’s, in the zone. Flares and tidal locking make a surviving atmosphere the whole question. A dense sky would open a deck. No such sky has been shown.

A spectrum is not a sample. These five are where the extrasolar prior can be aimed. They do not raise it to a fact.

## Flip condition

The prior rises if a deck is shown to persist, confine, and carry a gradient and carbon, and a sample returns a chemical system that is not lifted contamination. It falls if every temperate deck is a short-lived aerosol with no confinement. A cell in a Jovian droplet would make the solar-system case a fact. Until then the extrasolar dense-sky case is the cleaner theoretical bet, because the ground can be stipulated as hostile and the deck as the only buffered layer. A surface origin, or a well origin, on a rocky body does not flip this page. They are different bodies.

## Closing

A rock confines. A deck would have to park. Where a pressure-equilibrium layer is temperate, persistent, and able to hold a droplet or a membrane against dilution, aerostatic assembly is a prior worth having. In this system that layer is real on the gas giants and discussable on Venus, and the confinement is unshown. Earth’s clouds are the control that fails. On a dense-sky world elsewhere, the deck may be the well. Until a sample says otherwise, it is a structure, not a biology.

Further development, formalization, and testing are invited.
