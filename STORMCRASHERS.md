## Purpose

This document coalesces the Stormcrashers family into one implementable architecture: a permanent **100 m Windbreaker Tower** for localized atmospheric energy dissipation, and a **Coastal Hydro-Fractal Pylon Grid** for nearshore wave and surge attenuation. Both articles translate a marine wavebreaker into a porous, multi-scale dissipator rather than a solid wall.

This file is an **architecture and campaign document**. It is not a stamped construction set, not a bid package, and not a permit application. Member forces, tendon counts, pile-cap moments, and accumulator volumes are builder outputs after geotech and A0/B0. A builder who pours from the narrative alone has built a sculpture.

The architecture is offered as a supporting technical instrument within the [Humai Accord](README.md). It is consistent with [*Necessary Entropy*](NECESSARY_ENTROPY.md), [*Why Walk When You Can Ride?*](WHY_WALK_WHEN_YOU_CAN_RIDE.md), [*Empirical Demonstrations*](EMPIRICAL_DEMONSTRATIONS.md), the [Simulation Model of the Exterior Viability Protocol](SIMULATION_EXTERIOR_VIABILITY.md), the **Utilization Integrity Protocol**, and [The Call to Code](THE_CALL_TO_CODE.md). Scale-out follows the same counting doctrine used in the [Regenerative Lattice Core](REGENERATIVE_LATTICE_CORE.md): add sealed kits, do not invent a growable monolith.

It is not a claim that weather can be switched off, that a hurricane can be dismantled by one tower, or that a fractal tip stores residual or somatic signatures. Dual-use jet, mesh, and duct hardware is governed by the **Utilization Integrity Protocol**: this document describes protective dissipation only. Directed-energy, disintegration, and atmospheric-heating modes are excluded.

All performance bands below are provisional starting envelopes drawn from published windbreak, UHPC-tower, ducted-turbine, perforated-breakwater, suction-caisson, and bubble-curtain literature. They are orientation arithmetic for builders, not certified ratings. Campaigns A0–A1 and B0–B1 exist so those envelopes can be replaced by co-verified measurements.

## Core Design Principles (Humai Alignment)

1. **[*Necessary Entropy*](NECESSARY_ENTROPY.md)** — Multi-scale porosity is deliberate, bounded, and logged. It exists to convert coherent kinetic structure into uncorrelated small eddies, not to maximize novelty of form.
2. **Dissipate, do not dam** — Solid walls create short, violent lee recirculation. Porous articles lengthen the sheltered zone and lower peak load. This is the wavebreaker lesson applied to air and water.
3. **Passive first, active second** — The article must still function if compressors, jets, and turbines are locked out. Active layers are gated assists.
4. **Co-verification** — No shelter or $K_t$ claim is accepted from a single sensor class. Anemometry / wave staffs, structural-load cells, and independent far-field stations must agree within defined bands.
5. **[*Why Walk When You Can Ride?*](WHY_WALK_WHEN_YOU_CAN_RIDE.md)** — Recover energy from flow that is already being processed. Do not add a separate power plant whose only job is to feed the jets.
6. **Modularity and exit** — A unit, a line, or a ring can be added, isolated, or retired without collapsing the grid. Shipping channels remain open in ambient mode.
7. **Non-domination** — Siting, operation, and shutdown remain under local civil authority. The architecture does not create a weather monopoly.
8. **Utilization integrity** — Protective dissipation only. Hardware that can be retasked is still published as a shelter article; retasking is out of scope and out of license intent.

## Physical Premise

### Wavebreaker, translated

In deep water, wave energy is carried by orbital particle motion whose radius decays with depth:

$$
r = r_0 e^{kz},\qquad k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
$$

A wavebreaker does four things at once: it interrupts those orbits, scatters the incident potential ($\Phi_I$) into diffracted and radiated components ($\Phi_D$, $\Phi_R$), sheds vortices at edges, and — if tuned — oscillates out of phase with the crest. The useful product is a calmer wake, not a dry wall.

Air is not a free surface. Atmospheric “waves” of engineering interest here are gust fronts, shear layers, downbursts, and the planetary-boundary-layer turbulence that damages approach paths, cranes, and façades. The analog of orbital interruption is **blockage plus porosity**: force a coherent high-energy parcel through a multi-scale mesh so that its integral length scale collapses and viscous dissipation can finish the work.

Two scaling facts govern every later number.

- Density. Standard air at sea level is $\rho_a \approx 1.225\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. Seawater is $\rho_w \approx 1025\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. The same velocity produces roughly 800 times the dynamic pressure in water. Coastal members are therefore thicker, shorter, and foundation-dominated. Atmospheric members are taller and porosity-dominated.
- Open boundary. Wind can go over and around a single object. A lone 100 m tower is a point disruptor plus a short wake, not a regional climate machine. Useful shelter is an **array property**.

### What one unit can honestly do

A 100 m porous tower of optical porosity $\phi \approx 0.40\text{–}0.50$ is expected, by direct analogy to field windbreaks, to produce a measurable mean-speed reduction in a leeward belt on the order of $10H$ to $20H$ (about 1–2 km for $H = 100\,\mathrm{m}$), with the deepest reduction near $4H$ to $8H$. Solid fences reduce more immediately behind the barrier and recover sooner; porous fences protect farther. That is the design target. A *single* tower is weaker than a long belt of the same height; A0 and A1 exist to measure that difference rather than assume the agricultural-shelterbelt curve.

A single coastal pylon of comparable porosity is a pile / perforated-breakwater element. Published transmission coefficients for perforated and pile breakwaters commonly fall in $K_t \approx 0.5\text{–}0.8$ depending on steepness, spacing, and freeboard. Grids, not isolated pylons, produce harbor-scale attenuation.

Neither article is a hurricane switch.

## Article A — The 100 m Windbreaker Tower

### Stack

```
100 m    NiTi / titanium multi-scale tip + Coandă slots + sensor dome
 90 m    tensegrity-to-tip ring
 60 m    UHPC / tensegrity interface deck (venturi throat, turbines, winches)
  0 m    UHPC hollow mast, armor skin, utility trunk
 −2 m    pile cap / ring beam
−25 to −45 m   foundation piles (site-specific)
```

The 60 / 40 split is structural, not mystical. The lower two-thirds is a conventional post-tensioned UHPC wind-tower mast — a class already studied near 100 m for multi-megawatt turbines. The upper third is a light, tunable tensegrity that carries the porous tip and is allowed limited sway so that peak bending is not dumped entirely into the mast base.

### Layer 1 — Foundation

**Function.** Resist overturning from drag on the porous stack plus any brief jet-reaction loads. Survive debris and ice. Provide a path under $5\,\Omega$ to ground (target under $3\,\Omega$ where soil permits).

**Reference envelope (to be resized by geotech).**
- Pile group or drilled shafts under a ring cap; typical working depth 25–45 m in competent soils. Sixteen-pile concentric layouts are a starting pattern, not a mandate.
- Cap and shafts in sulfate-resistant concrete or geopolymer with corrosion-protected tendons. UHPC or HPC in the splash / soil-cut zone.
- Lightning: copper or copper-clad ring bonding every shaft to a common electrode field. Chemical backfill only where local code and groundwater chemistry allow; do not specify a proprietary electrolytic leaching system as required.
- Optional below-grade vault: compressed-air accumulators for the Coandă manifold and a fire-water reserve. Size the air store from the first-article jet duty below, then revise after A0.

**Design parameters to lock in the geotech package.**
- 50-year and 500-year 3-second gust at hub / tip height (ASCE 7 / local equivalent)
- Directional drag $C_D(\phi,\alpha)$ of the as-built porous stack from wind-tunnel or CFD, not from a solid-cylinder default
- Soil $p$ – $y$ curves, liquefaction screen, frost depth, and scour if sited on fill
- Ice-throw and blade/mesh-fragment setback using the same planning screens as a 100 m wind-turbine tower (IEC 61400-series local adoption)

### Layer 2 — UHPC mast (0–60 m)

**Function.** Inertial spine, debris armor, vertical intake for the optional venturi, utility trunk.

**Material envelope (published).**
- Structural UHPC / UHPFRC: characteristic compression $f_c' \approx 150\text{–}200\,\mathrm{MPa}$ is the working band used in published hybrid wind-tower studies. Broader commercial UHPC spans about 120–250 MPa. Design tensile / first-crack values are far lower (6–15 MPa class); do not treat 200 MPa compression as if it were tensile capacity.
- Density about $2300\text{–}2800\,\mathrm{kg\,m}^{-3}$; $E \approx 40\text{–}50\,\mathrm{GPa}$.
- Post-tensioning: high-strength strand or CFRP tendons at a locked-off effective stress on the order of 1200 MPa (published UHPC-tower practice). Final tendon count is a calculation, not a slogan.

**Starting geometry (resize after load cases).**
- Outer diameter at base about 7–9 m, tapering if the venturi so requires
- Wall 0.8–1.3 m at the base, thinning upward within service-stress limits
- Inner bore fair to a circular intake; avoid sudden offsets that separate the core flow
- Armor: bolted steel or titanium-alloy panels on viscoelastic pads in the debris zone (0–30 m). Full ceramic additive skins are optional, not required for a first article. The mandatory property is impact resistance to local missile criteria (IBC / FEMA windborne-debris zone if applicable), not a brand of ceramic.

**Utilities trunk.** Dual diverse fiber paths, isolated power, and at least two physically separate controller rooms at different heights. Three-way voting is permitted; it is not a substitute for a spring-closed fail-safe on every damper and blade.

### Layer 3 — Tensegrity upper truss (60–100 m)

**Function.** Carry the tip, host the venturi throat, shift the stack’s natural frequency away from dominant shedding, and accept limited elastic sway.

**Members.**
- Compression: Ti-6Al-4V or equivalent high-strength steel hollow struts if titanium lead time dominates cost. Grade-5 titanium is preferred at the tip interface for corrosion and fatigue; steel is acceptable lower in the truss if the corrosion package is honest.
- Tension: high-modulus carbon or aramid cables, pre-tensioned to a defined fraction of ultimate (starting band 35–50% UTS) by deck winches.
- Interface deck at 60 m: steel / composite ring that lands the strand-jacked generation core and the cable winches.

**Sway parameter.** Design for a controlled tip rotation on the order of several degrees under extreme load, not a theatrical large angle as a requirement. The useful property is a tunable frequency, not the angle itself.

**Vortex-shedding lock-in.** Keep reduced velocity $V / (f_n D)$ out of the lock-in band for the mast and for the tip’s major length scales. Cable tension is the cheap actuator; jets are the expensive one.

### Layer 4 — Multi-scale tip

**Function.** Present a porous, multi-length-scale obstacle to incoming coherent structures, and host the Coandă slots and sensors.

**Geometry rule.** A Sierpinski-derived tetrahedral frame plus a Cantor-like pore-size cascade is one legal way to pack several length scales into one object. The physics that matters is the **porosity profile with height and the edge-length spectrum**, not the brand name of the fractal. Builders may substitute any lattice whose optical porosity in the working wind window is

$$
\phi = 1 - \frac{A_{\mathrm{solid}}}{A_{\mathrm{envelope}}} \approx 0.40\text{–}0.50
$$

with pore / gap sizes spanning at least two decades (centimetres to millimetres). That span is what collapses integral length scales.

**Skin.** Perforated titanium or coated stainless sheet, or a structural mesh on the NiTi / titanium frame. Superhydrophobic or ice-phobic finish in freezing climates. Embedded CNT or resistive heating plus piezoelectric de-ice at nodes is the winter package; pneumatic purge of the pores is a third line, not the first.

**NiTi role.** Superelastic NiTi is used where high-cycle local flex and vibration isolation pay for themselves (tip struts, mesh carriers). It is not a weather-eating alloy. Quote transformation temperatures and fatigue data from the mill certificate; do not treat “shape memory” as a load path.

**Coandă assist (gated) — first-article duty envelope.** Circumferential slot nozzles on the tip and upper ring. Purpose: attach a thin sheet of air to a curved surface so that the effective turning of the external flow increases without presenting a solid sail. This is circulation control, documented on airfoils and façades. It is not a supersonic wall.

Until A0 supplies a measured blowing map, size the first article to the following **placeholder duty** and then replace the numbers:

- Blowing coefficient target band: $C_{\mu} \approx 0.01\text{–}0.08$ on the tip reference area, not a continuous high - $C_{\mu}$ curtain
- Pulse length: 5–15 s per gust passage
- Event budget: on the order of 10–100 pulses per named storm; a handful of named storms per year at a mid-latitude site
- Slot pressure after regulation: low (single-digit to low-tens of bar). Accumulators may sit at 300–350 bar *upstream* of regulators; the slot does not see bottle pressure
- Sector count: 10–100 independently valved sectors
- Valve close-time: tens of milliseconds is adequate
- House-load check: ambient duct plus stored air must cover the annual pulse budget; if it cannot, shrink the duty rather than add a dedicated fuel generator
- Hard interlock: jets inhibit if accumulators are below a set pressure, if icing is detected on the slots, if $C_{\mu}$ demand would exceed the placeholder band, or if the structure is in construction / service mode

A0 may raise or kill the jet layer. If the passive tip already meets the $5H$ / $15H$ pass criterion, jets remain optional.

### Layer 5 — Sensing and control

**Minimum viable set.**
- 3-D sonic anemometers at 10 m, 60 m, tip, and at least two independent leeward masts at about $5H$ and $15H$
- Pressure taps on the tip faces
- Strain / cable-tension monitoring at the 60 m ring and mast base
- Lightning-current and grounding-impedance log
- Optional: short-range Doppler lidar or X-band for incoming gust fronts (range of a few kilometres is useful; a long advertised range is a purchase option, not a physics requirement)
- Acoustic: at least one community microphone or sound-level logger at the nearest receptor, 10-minute $L_{\mathrm{eq}}$ during jet pulses and rotor operation

**Control law, first generation.** Look-up plus gain-scheduled tension and jet maps from wind-tunnel / CFD, updated by model-predictive control only after the passive article has a measured baseline. Physics-informed networks are permitted later. They are not the commissioning controller.

**Fail-safe.** Loss of power or loss of heartbeat dumps cable winches to a pre-set storm tension, closes jet valves by spring or stored-air trip, and feathers or brakes any turbine. The article becomes a dumb porous tower. That is the correct degraded mode.

### Optional energy recovery — ducted venturi

The hollow mast can be a diffuser-augmented intake.

Continuity in incompressible form (adequate for these Mach numbers):

$$
A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2
$$

A 2:1 diameter contraction (8 m to 4 m) implies a 4 times area ratio and a 4 times ideal velocity rise before losses. Real ducts separate. Do not budget the ideal multiplier.

Power in the free stream:

$$
P_{\mathrm{wind}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho A V^3
$$

Report two coefficients, always:
- $C_{P,\mathrm{rotor}}$ on throat area
- $C_{P,\mathrm{device}}$ on intake / envelope area

Open-rotor Betz–Joukowsky limit is $16/27 \approx 0.593$. Ducted machines can exceed that limit **on rotor area** and sometimes on device area; published experimental ducted $C_{P,\mathrm{rotor}}$ values often land near 0.5–0.9 depending on how $A$ is defined. A planning band of $C_{P,\mathrm{device}} \approx 0.30\text{–}0.50$ in good ambient winds is honest for a first vertical-duct stack. Do not publish a single high coefficient as a measured fact.

Starting mechanical package:
- One to three stages of ducted rotor, counter-rotating if yaw is fixed
- Permanent-magnet generators
- Resistive brake sized to the electrical stall load, air-cooled in the bypass
- Iris or louver that chokes the throat in survival winds so the rotors are not the fuse

Ambient-mode energy is for house load, accumulators, and de-ice. It is not the economic justification of the tower. The justification is avoided damage in a defined lee plus, secondarily, operations energy.

### Acoustic and ice-throw planning parameters

Use wind-turbine practice as the starting screen, then measure.

- Night lockout for jets and ducted rotors unless the measured receptor $L_{\mathrm{eq}}$ stays inside the local ordinance. Treat valve chatter as a design load, not a surprise.
- Ice-throw and mesh-fragment setback: plan as for a 100 m turbine of similar top diameter until a site-specific trajectory study exists. De-ice before a forecast icing event rather than throwing ice on purpose.
- These are siting parameters for A1, not A0 tunnel products.

## Article B — Coastal Hydro-Fractal Pylon

### Role

A shorter, heavier cousin of the Windbreaker, sited on 15–30 m shelves seaward of a harbor, inlet, or low-lying district. It processes an incompressible free-surface flow. The same porosity doctrine applies: let water through, tear the orbital structure, dump energy into turbulence and heat.

### Stack (typical, water depth $h \approx 20\,\mathrm{m}$)

```
+8 to +12 m    freeboard head / navigation mark / sensor mast
  0 m          mean water — working hydro-fractal collar in the wave zone
 −h            caisson lid / pile cap
 −h−8 to −h−15 m   suction-caisson skirt or pile embedment (site-specific)
```

Do not copy the 100 m air tower underwater. Wave energy lives near the surface. Put the porous working face in the wave zone and keep the foundation compact.

### Foundation

Suction caissons in sand and many clays are a documented shallow-water option (published prototype and farm geometries commonly 8–16 m diameter, skirt $L/D$ often about 0.5–1.5 in shallow sites). Alternatives: monopile, jacket-on-piles, gravity base. Choose from the geotech campaign. Scour protection is mandatory.

### Working face

- Multi-scale perforated lattice (the “hydro-fractal”) spanning the wave zone, optical / geometric porosity again in the 0.3–0.5 band unless tank tests say otherwise
- Member scale from pile-diameter class down to perforations of centimetres
- NiTi or duplex stainless at the high-cycle wet/dry collar; UHPC or coated steel below
- Optional internal venturi / tidal throat for ambient energy recovery (report $C_P$ the same dual-area way)

### Active layers (gated)

1. **Water Coandă / jet curtain.** High-volume slots that throw a sheet along the outer curve to trip breaking seaward of the protected zone. Duty cycle is surge-peak only. First-article placeholder: one documented specific discharge from B0; no continuous pump-against-the-ocean mode.
2. **Bubble curtain.** Air injection upstream of the lattice. Bubble curtains are field-proven for acoustic attenuation and have documented, smaller effects on wave shape and near-field currents. Treat them as a density-and-compressibility modifier and a noise mitigator during construction, not as a device that collapses a design surge by decree. Size compressors from void-fraction targets measured in a flume, not from a slogan.

### Biofouling package (required)

A plugged mesh is a solid sail. Three stacked lines, all documented in marine practice:
- Foul-release or zwitterionic / hydrogel coating on the working mesh
- Periodic low-voltage local chlorination from the copper mesh **only** if discharge permits allow it; keep the reaction in the millimetre boundary layer
- Ultrasonic / mechanical clean on a scheduled interval; 20–40 kHz class transducers are the same family used for ice and fouling elsewhere

Target: keep open area within a few percent of as-built over the service life. Measure it. Do not claim a near-perfect open-area fraction for 25 years without a log.

### Navigation and ecology

- Marked, lit, AIS-visible
- Ambient-mode openness for fish passage and residual longshore transport; the grid is not a seawall
- Construction-phase bubble curtain as a mammal-noise control where pile driving occurs
- No tributyltin; no continuous heavy-metal leach as the primary antifoul

## Array Doctrine and Scalability

Shelter is an array property. Scale by counting sealed articles, the same way the [Regenerative Lattice Core](REGENERATIVE_LATTICE_CORE.md) scales by counting cassettes.

### Atmospheric patterns

| Pattern | Units | Intended product | Notes |
|---------|-------|------------------|-------|
| Point | 1 | Instrument + short wake; protect a pad or crane | First article. Do not sell city-scale shelter. |
| Line | 3–12 | Leeward belt for a runway, tank farm, or town edge | Spacing $2H$ to $6H$ **pending A0**; stagger if yaw varies. |
| Staggered belt | 2–3 rows | Longer, more uniform lee | Porosity of the *belt* matters more than porosity of one tip. |
| Perimeter | site-specific | Facility wrap | Leave upwind gaps so you do not create a solid-wall horseshoe vortex. |

Rule of thumb from agricultural and fence windbreaks, pending site CFD and A0:
- Useful mean-speed cut to about $15H$ – $25H$ leeward at $\phi \approx 0.40\text{–}0.50$
- Deepest cut near $4H$ – $8H$
- Windward influence $2H$ – $5H$

A 100 m line of ten towers is a 1–2 km instrumented fence, not a climate program. Do not detach the “pending A0” clause from the spacing band in slide decks.

### Coastal patterns

| Pattern | Units | Intended product | Notes |
|---------|-------|------------------|-------|
| Single pylon | 1 | Calibration + navigation mark | Tank-validate $K_t$ here. |
| Outer shock line | 8–20 | First cut of long swell | Spacing from wavelength and $D$; start near $1D$ – $3D$ clear gap **pending B0**. |
| Inner gate line | 6–16 | Residual surge / wind-sea | May carry the jet / bubble layer. |
| Dual ring | outer + inner | Harbor or inlet | Keep a navigation gate with active closure only under forecast. |

Published perforated-pile and curtain-wall-pile devices often report $K_t$ in the 0.5–0.8 band for a single row, lower for optimized perforated heads and multiple rows. A planning target of “incident $H_s$ cut by about one-third to one-half across a dual line, for the design steepness band” is a campaign goal. It is not a warranty.

### Growth path of the two parent articles

1. Campaign A0 / B0 (tunnel and flume), including pore-Re and sediment rules.
2. One onshore prototype beside an already-instrumented industrial pad (A1).
3. One coastal prototype in a permitted test lease with a wave-rider pair (B1).
4. A three-to-five unit atmospheric line protecting a real asset.
5. A dual-line coastal gate on a secondary inlet before any claim on a metropolitan bay.
6. Replication by counting kits. Do not invent a growable tower that changes class mid-life.

### Later-family horizon (undefined on purpose)

The parent articles are point and line dissipators. If A1 and B1 produce co-verified wakes, the same porosity doctrine can be asked of other geometries. Those geometries are **named here only so the scalability ladder is visible**. They are not specified, rated, or promised in this file.

## Campaign A0 — Wind-Tunnel / LES Matrix

Purpose: replace borrowed shelterbelt curves with a $C_D(\phi,\alpha)$ map and a lee-speed field for *this* tip-and-mast geometry before anyone pours UHPC.

**Model.** Rigid tip + mast, scale 1:80 to 1:150. Blockage at or under 5% preferred; apply a published blockage correction if higher. Surface roughness scaled or tripped.

**Pore-Re decision rule.** Define a pore Reynolds number on the dominant gap:

$$
\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{pore}} = \frac{V\,d_{\mathrm{pore}}}{\nu}
$$

Log prototype $\mathrm{Re_{pore}}$ and model $\mathrm{Re_{pore}}$ for every speed. If they are not within about one order of magnitude, **do not freeze working $\phi$ from the tunnel**. Treat $\phi$ as a field variable until A1. Tunnel $C_D$ and lee maps remain usable as qualitative ranking across the $\phi$ set, not as permit numbers.

**Optical porosity set (required).**

$$
\phi \in \{0.30,\ 0.35,\ 0.40,\ 0.45,\ 0.50,\ 0.55,\ 0.60\}
$$

Build $\phi$ by opening the same lattice family, not by swapping unrelated silhouettes. Photograph each model against a backlight and compute $\phi$ from the image as well as from CAD.

**Yaw set (required).**

$$
\alpha \in \{0^{\circ},\ \pm 10^{\circ},\ \pm 20^{\circ},\ \pm 30^{\circ},\ \pm 40^{\circ}\}
$$

Add $\pm 15^{\circ}$ and $\pm 25^{\circ}$ only if the $C_D$ curve is non-monotonic between the required points.

**Speed set.** At least three tunnel speeds spanning the scaled service and survival windows. One run per $(\phi,\alpha,V)$ cell.

**Minimum cell count.** $7 \times 9 \times 3 = 189$ forced-balance points before repeats. Budget 10% repeats for drift.

**Instrumentation.**
- Six-component base balance, calibrated the same day
- Hot-wire or Cobra / IR probes on a traverse at $x/H = 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 15, 20$ and $z/H = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0$
- Base and tip surface pressures on at least one face
- Unsteady moment spectrum for shedding $f_s$
- Optional: a blowing-slot add-on at working $\phi$ only, swept across the $C_{\mu}$ placeholder band

**Deliverables (A0 close-out package).**
- $C_D(\phi,\alpha)$ and $C_M(\phi,\alpha)$ tables
- Lee-speed ratio $U(x,z)/U_{\infty}$ maps
- Shedding Strouhal vs. $\phi$
- Recommended working $\phi$ for longest $0.8U_{\infty}$ recovery distance — flagged “ranking only” if the pore-Re rule failed
- Iced-skin sensitivity: one extra model at working $\phi$ with pores 50% blocked
- Jet-on vs jet-off delta at working $\phi$, or a written decision that jets stay optional
- CFD / LES validation against the same matrix before any full-scale CFD is used for permits

**Pass criterion (provisional).** At working $\phi$, a single-tower lee shows a measurable mean-speed cut at $5H$ and a still-measurable cut at $15H$ in at least the $\alpha = 0^{\circ}$ and $\pm 20^{\circ}$ cuts. If the $15H$ cut vanishes, the first article is an instrumented point disruptor only; do not sell a belt.

## Campaign B0 — Flume Matrix

Purpose: replace borrowed $K_t$ bands with measured transmission, reflection, and dissipation for one pylon and a three-unit line.

**Model scale.** 1:30 to 1:50 in a 2-D flume for the single unit; 3-D basin for the three-unit line if available.

**Porosity set.** $\phi \in \{0.30,\ 0.40,\ 0.50\}$ on the working collar.

**Layout set.**
- Isolated pylon
- Three-unit line at clear gaps $1D$, $2D$, $3D$

**Sea-state set (irregular, JONSWAP or local spectrum).**
- $H_s$ scaled to prototype 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, and 4.0 m
- $T_p$ spanning wind-sea and swell bands for the target shelf
- At least two water levels (mean and design high)

**Active-layer set.**
- Passive lattice only
- Lattice + metered bubble fraction (two void-fraction targets from a separate bubble-only run)
- Lattice + jet curtain at one documented specific discharge

**Sediment / scour rule (B0+).** After a one-hour prototype-equivalent run at the design sea state:
- Record bed elevation to at least $5D$ around each pile / caisson
- **Fail the gap or the foot protection** if local scour depth exceeds 20% of local water depth or if a deposition lobe grows beyond $3D$ in a way that would steer the next line’s inflow
- A dual-line concept does not pass B0 on $K_t$ alone

**Deliverables.** $K_t$, $K_r$, $K_d$ vs. $H_s/L_p$, gap, $\phi$, and active-layer state; collar force / moment; scour / deposition map. Energy check $K_t^{2} + K_r^{2} + K_d^{2} \approx 1$ within flume tolerance.

**Pass criterion (provisional).** Isolated $K_t$ lands inside 0.5–0.8 for the design steepness band. The three-unit line beats the isolated unit. Bubble / jet layers are kept only if they beat passive by more than the run-to-run scatter. Sediment rule is a separate pass.

## Assembly Sequence — Windbreaker

Borrow the hybrid method used on tall concrete wind masts: heavy crane low, strand-jack high.

**Phase 0.** Site met, geotech, unexploded-ordnance, utilities, acoustic receptor survey, ice-throw setback, and a construction-phase porous berm (optical porosity about 0.4) around the pad. Solid site hoardings create the downdraft the finished tower is designed to avoid.

**Phase 1 — foundation and lower rings (0 to about 35 m).** All-terrain crane in the 100–200 t at-radius class positions precast UHPC rings (segment mass is a design output; 100–160 t is a plausible band for 5 m lifts at this diameter). Vertical tendons stressed in stages.

**Phase 2 — mast to 60 m and core jack.** Assemble the venturi / turbine cassette at grade inside the bore. Strand-jack to the 60 m shelf and lock. Auxiliary crane handles only light decks.

**Phase 3 — tensegrity.** Temporary central shore. Place struts. String cables. Tension to the lock-off band until the shore unloads. Remove shore.

**Phase 4 — tip.** Ground-build the tip as one module. Lift only in a defined wind window (starting lockout: gusts above about $7\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ at hook height unless tag-line thrusters are qualified). Pin, torque, connect pneumatics and fiber. Use specified structural fasteners and qualified welds. No exotic field-fusion process is required.

**Commissioning gates.**
- G0: as-built survey and tendon log
- G1: 24 h ambient anemometry vs. two lee masts
- G2: controlled winch-sweep of cable tension vs. measured $f_n$
- G3: accumulator / jet pulse on a calm day, with receptor $L_{\mathrm{eq}}$
- G4: first-season storm log against the co-verification rule

## Instrumentation, Calibration, and Co-verification

No shelter or transmission claim enters the public log unless at least two of the three classes agree.

| Class | Atmospheric article | Coastal article |
|-------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Flow | Sonic anemometers + optional lidar | Wave staffs / buoys seaward and leeward; ADCP |
| Load | Base moment, cable tension, tip acceleration | Collar strain, caisson tilt, pore-pressure |
| Far field | Independent mast or sodar off the owner’s network | Third-party tide / wave station |

Report reductions as bands with sample size and stability class (or sea state), not as a single percentage.

### Calibration minima

- Sonic anemometers: manufacturer or wind-tunnel calibration current within 12 months; boom alignment logged; $20\,\mathrm{Hz}$ or better raw store during campaigns
- Wave staffs / buoys: still-water zero and range check on deployment day; clock sync to UTC
- Load cells / cable load pins: deadweight or reference-cell check at install and annually
- Pressure taps: manometer or calibrator traceable to a secondary standard
- Electrical power (if $C_P$ is claimed): revenue-grade or calibrated power analyzer; air density from co-located $T$, $p$, RH
- Sound level: Type 1 or Type 2 meter, field check the same day as G3
- Lightning / earth: fall-of-potential or equivalent; log after each stroke event
- Flume / tunnel balances: same-day shunt or known-weight check

### Dual-metric rule when energy recovery is claimed

- Always publish $C_{P,\mathrm{rotor}}$ and $C_{P,\mathrm{device}}$
- Always publish house-load fraction vs. export
- Never net jet energy against a storm that was not measured
- Ambient $C_P$ and storm-mode brake energy are separate rows

## Safety Envelopes

**Atmospheric survival.** Design to the local 500-year 3-second gust at tip height, iced and un-iced, plus missile criteria in debris zones. Cable-loss case: the mast alone must stand. Jet-on case: include reaction and the failed-open sail case (slots iced shut).

**Coastal survival.** Design wave with depth-limited breaking on the collar, plus current plus wind on the dry head. Caisson installation vacuum and in-place reverse-end-bearing checks are part of the envelope, not extras.

**Electrical.** Faraday / bonding path sized for a $200\,\mathrm{kA}$-class stroke to ground, coordinated with local code. Carbon tension members must not be the lightning path.

**Human.** No occupied cabin at height in storm mode. Drone or climber inspection only below a published wind lockout. Ice-throw and fragment setbacks as for a 100 m turbine until a site study exists.

**Cyber.** Authenticated command path; local fail-safe does not wait for a cloud vote.

**Utilization.** Protective dissipation only, under the **[Utilization Integrity Protocol](UTILIZATION_INTEGRITY_PROTOCOL.md)**. No residual-flux, somatic, or harmonic-experience experiment rides on this structure.

## Worked Orientation Arithmetic

Not a rating. A check that the energy story is dimensionally sane.

**Lee-side windbreak.** For $H = 100\,\mathrm{m}$, $\phi = 0.45$, a $20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ approach:

- Fence literature: near-lee speeds often 0.25–0.50 of open-field at $4H$ – $6H$ for mid-porosity *belts*, recovering toward 0.8 by about $20H$ – $25H$.
- A planning expectation for a *single* tower is weaker than a long belt. Log it as such. A0 is the correction.

**Duct power, ambient.** Intake $D = 8\,\mathrm{m}$, $A_1 \approx 50\,\mathrm{m}^{2}$, $V = 8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$:

$$
P_{\mathrm{wind}} = \tfrac{1}{2}(1.225)(50)(8^{3}) \approx 15.7\,\mathrm{kW}
$$

At $C_{P,\mathrm{device}} = 0.40$, electrical order 6 kW before losses — house load, not a power station. Storm-mode flow is braked, not harvested as a boast.

**Coastal $K_t$.** Incident $H_s = 3\,\mathrm{m}$, single-row $K_t = 0.65$ gives transmitted about 2.0 m. A second row at $K_t = 0.70$ on the residual gives about 1.4 m. That is a meaningful harbor number. It is not a decree that a design surge collapses to a ripple.

**Jet store, first-article placeholder.** A 10 s pulse at modest $C_{\mu}$ on a tip of tens of square metres of reference area is an accumulator problem, not a pipeline problem. If the annual pulse budget cannot be charged from the ambient duct, cut sectors or pulse length before adding fuel.

## Environmental and Siting Parameters

- Bird / bat: treat the tip as a structure with small-gap mesh; follow local wind-energy siting screens. Night lighting is navigation-minimum.
- Shadow flicker and ice throw: same screens as a 100 m turbine tower until a site trajectory study exists.
- Noise: ducted rotors and jets are intermittent; night lockout unless G3 clears the receptor.
- Coastal sediment: B0+ pass/fail, then a first-season survey. Porous is better than a wall; it is not invisible.
- Public realm: the article is infrastructure. Publish the co-verified logs.
- Exterior viability: an array that shelters one pad by starving a downwind community of wind or by closing a fishery is a failed siting, even if $C_D$ looks elegant. Use the local-global reading in the [Exterior Viability](SIMULATION_EXTERIOR_VIABILITY.md) model before a multi-unit permit.
