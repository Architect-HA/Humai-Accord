# Life Wells
### *Subsurface Assembly at the Sump, Before a Surface Is Ready*
#### *A comprehensive analysis of the viability of subsurface biology across the various terrestrial bodies of the Solar System*
---
by **Bradford James Focht** (The Architect / Aspenth)  
*v1.0 - v1.8 — October 18th, 2026*<br>

---

## Purpose

This document is a general theory of where life is most likely to assemble on a terrestrial body, and a worked reading of the bodies in this system that are large enough to have had an interior.

The claim is narrow. While a surface is still swinging in temperature and in atmospheric composition, and while it is open to the raw star, it is a poor cradle. A more stable cradle is a subsurface or transitional sump: the place where water, runoff, and dissolved chemistry meet denser rock in thin, high-pressure fissures, buffered from the star. Life may form there, persist there, and never emerge.

Bodies treated here begin with the planets, which are the best-studied cases, and then the moons and smaller rocky bodies that have a measured solvent or a measured reason not to.

Pluto is counted as a planet. The dwarf-planet label is a catalog choice, not a physical one. Ceres and Vesta are the two large rocky bodies of the belt that Dawn measured. Icy moons are in scope where they have a well. A bare rock with no solvent is named and passed over.

It is not a detection. It is a prior, and a map of where a later campaign would look.

---

## The general case

A terrestrial body has a surface and an interior. The surface is the face the star sees. Early in a body’s life that face is a poor reactor.

- Temperature swings are large. A day can freeze what a noon thawed.
- The atmosphere, if any, is still being built, stripped, or replaced.
- Radiation and impact gardening rework the top meters.
- Water, if present, is episodic at the surface. A pond that exists for a season is a pond that dries.

None of this forbids surface assembly. It makes it a race against the swing and against the star. A cradle that does not see the star does not run that race.

### The sump

Water moves down. In rock that has fractures, caves, or a porous crust, it collects where the pathways thin. That collection is the well.

At the sump three things meet that a surface pond does not reliably offer.

1. **Persistence.** A fissure at depth is buffered. The diurnal swing does not arrive in full.
2. **Confinement.** Thin pathways and pressure hold reactants together. A mineral surface is a wall and a catalyst.
3. **Chemistry.** Runoff and the rock concentrate as the path thins. A sump is a natural still.

The well is not required to be hot. Warm is enough if the chemistry is reducing and the water stays. A cold brine vein is a thinner case of the same idea. A transitional zone, just below the gardened surface but not yet in the deep crust, counts if it is buffered from the star.

### What has to be true for assembly

Publicly grounded requirements, not a recipe.

- A solvent. Water is the one we know works. A nitrogen or ammonia melt is a different chemistry and a lower prior.
- A redox gradient. Hydrogen from rock–water reactions, iron, sulfur, or a radiolytic product.
- Carbon, or a substitute we have not demonstrated. CO₂, carbonate, or organic infall.
- Time, longer than the surface swing. A season is not a well.
- Confinement. A mineral pore, a fissure, a micropore. Open ocean is a later habitat, not the first still.

**Energy and carbon, as measured classes.** A well without a gradient is a puddle. The gradients we can point at are serpentinization (hydrogen from olivine and water), radiolysis of water by radioactive decay, and redox pairs in iron and sulfur minerals. Carbon arrives as CO₂, as carbonate, or as organic infall. A body whose rock is olivine-poor and whose carbon is missing is a poorer well. Vesta is that case. Ceres is the opposite.

Earth shows that once these exist underground, life can fill them. Earth does not show that they are where life began. A warm fracture remains as live a candidate as a warm pond. Alkaline-vent claims are influential and contested. The origin site is unresolved.

### Emergence

Assembly and emergence are different. A well can hold a biosphere for as long as water, a gradient, and a carbon source persist. Emergence onto a surface requires that surface to become acceptable. A body can be alive underground and sterile on the face. That is the case this analysis is written to allow.

### Why the other cradles lose

While a surface is open to the star and still building its sky, the usual alternatives each fail a named requirement. This is the comparative claim. It is not a proof that a well assembled life.

- **A warm pond.** Fails persistence. A pond that freezes, dries, or is gardened every day does not hold a reactant set long enough. A sump does not see that swing.
- **Open ocean.** Fails confinement. Reactants dilute. A pore or a fissure holds them. An ocean is a later habitat, once assembly has somewhere to have happened.
- **An aerosol or a cloud droplet.** Fails time and confinement. A droplet is a brief reactor. Venus’s cloud deck is the test case, and it is disputed. It is not the prior.
- **An impact melt.** Fails persistence. An impact makes heat and a transient fluid. It does not make a standing well unless the melt feeds a fracture that outlives the crater.

A cradle that keeps solvent, a gradient, carbon, and confinement through the swing wins the comparison. That is the well. A later, stable surface can inherit. It is a bad place to start.

### Flip condition

The prior flips if a body with a swinging, irradiated, drying surface is shown to have assembled life there, and a matched body with a long-lived sump of solvent, gradient, carbon, and time is shown sterile. A sump that is systematically sterile under those four conditions weakens the assembly claim. A surface origin on Earth alone does not flip it. Earth is one case, and its origin site is unresolved.

## Mercury

**Face.** No real atmosphere. Dayside temperatures exceed $400\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. Nightside falls below $-170\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. The swing is the whole story of the surface.

**Interior and ices.** MESSENGER found water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters, on the order of $10^{14}$ to $10^{15}\,\mathrm{kg}$, with a hydrogen-rich layer under a thinner cover. Some low-reflectance polar deposits track the stability line of heavy organics. The surface itself is volatile-rich for a terrestrial planet: elevated sulfur, carbon, sodium, potassium, and chlorine, low iron, high magnesium. Normative mineralogy is plagioclase, pyroxene, and olivine, plus sulfides.

**Well.** A polar cold trap is not a sump. It is a freezer. A fracture well would have to sit under a crust that has no persistent liquid and no atmosphere to feed it. Past volcanic and impact heat may have made transient fluids. Nothing measured says they lasted. Prior for a present well: low. Prior for a past, short-lived fracture fluid: possible, untestable from orbit.

---

## Venus

**Face.** Surface about $460\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and about $92\,\mathrm{bar}$. Atmosphere about $96.5$ percent CO₂ and $3.5$ percent nitrogen, with sulfur dioxide. Liquid water is not a surface condition. It has not been for a long time.

**Clouds.** Around $50\text{–}60\,\mathrm{km}$ the temperature and pressure approach Earth-like values. Phosphine has been reported and disputed. It is not a detection of life. A cloud habitat would be an aerial transitional zone, not a well. This page does not rest on it.

**Well.** Venus may once have had oceans. If it did, a fracture sump is the prior for any assembly, for the same reason as anywhere else. The present surface is a sterilizing oven to a depth that depends on the geotherm. A deep well, if the crust still holds a fluid, is the only present candidate, and it is unmeasured. Prior for a past well: the same as any early wet terrestrial body. Prior for persistence now: low, unless a drill finds fluid.

## Earth

**Face.** The one surface in this list that became acceptable. Liquid water, a thick atmosphere, a magnetic field, a swing life can cross.

**Interior.** A large fraction of microbial biomass may live underground. Deep fracture communities, mine waters, and subseafloor rock are inhabited. Some live on hydrogen from rock–water reactions. The deep biosphere is a census, not a hypothesis (Gold 1992; Colman and others 2017).

**Origin.** Unresolved. Pond, vent, crater, and fracture are all live. Life Wells takes the fracture as the prior for any body whose surface is still unstable, and does not pretend Earth has confirmed it.

### Earth sumps, as measured

The habitat claim rests on counts, not on a story.

- **Caves.** Oligotrophic, often under $2\,\mathrm{mg}$ of organic carbon per litre, and still on the order of $10^{6}$ cells per gram of rock. Proteobacteria and Actinobacteria dominate. Sulfuric caves run on sulfur-oxidizing biofilms that enlarge the cave. Epigenic caves are more diverse. Iron- and manganese-precipitating bacteria are known from cave pools and sumps. Carbonate speleothems are a mineral product of the same rooms.
- **Deep fractures.** Continental crust is estimated at $2\text{–}6\times 10^{29}$ cells, with a similar order in oceanic crust (Magnabosco and others 2018). Fracture water at a kilometer in granite carries distinct communities across sharp chemical gradients. Serpentinizing systems generate H₂ and can reduce inorganic carbon to methane and short organics. Cell counts there run from about $10^{1}$ to $10^{5}$ per millilitre in fluids, and from tens to $10^{7}$ per gram in altered rock. Hyperalkaline, highly reducing water is a stress. It is still inhabited.
- **What this does and does not show.** A sump can be mineral-rich, chemically graduated, and biologically dense with almost no surface organic input. That is viability as a habitat. It is not a demonstration that life assembled there. The origin question stays open. The richness is why the prior prefers a well over a swinging pond.

## Mars

**Face.** About $610\,\mathrm{Pa}$, about $95$ percent CO₂, mean temperature about $-60\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. No stable surface liquid water. Top meters gardened.

**History.** Noachian water, about $3.5$ to $4$ billion years ago. Valley networks, lakes, aqueous minerals. Gale shows clay giving way upward to hydrated magnesium sulfate. CheMin has found Fe/Mg-smectite, sulfates, hematite, and in places Fe-carbonate, gypsum, and halite. Nili Fossae and Mawrth carry phyllosilicates and carbonates. The atmosphere was then largely lost.

**Minerals that raise a well.** Smectite, kaolinite, nontronite. Sulfates and jarosite. Carbonates. Iron oxides. Perchlorate, which helps brine and stresses a cell. Olivine and pyroxene as serpentinization feedstock.

**Interior now.** Subsurface ice. Brines. Seasonal marsquakes read as near-surface brine in the north (2025). Thin liquid veins in permafrost, some wide enough on paper for a microbe. Deeper radiogenic habitats, including a modeled case at Acidalia Planitia. Methane reported and disputed.

**Well.** The prior cradle is a Noachian sump, not an open lake. Emergence required a surface Mars did not keep. Present candidates are brine veins, then deeper fracture water. Neither has been sampled for life.

## Pluto

**Face.** Nitrogen, methane, and water ice. Surface temperatures near $40\,\mathrm{K}$. A thin nitrogen atmosphere, about $11\,\mu\mathrm{bar}$ when New Horizons flew, in vapor equilibrium with the ice. Sputnik Planitia is a vast nitrogen-ice basin, geologically young.

**Interior.** A subsurface ocean is the working model. Fracture patterns at Sputnik are fit by an ocean under $40\text{–}80\,\mathrm{km}$ of ice, at most about $8$ percent denser than seawater. Recent work has argued for liquid nitrogen moving at the base of the glacier and reaching the surface in pulses. That is a cryovolcanic path, not a warm well.

**Well.** If the ocean is real, it is the well: a confined, persistent, salt-bearing water body under ice, buffered from the Sun. The solvent is water. The temperature is the open variable. A cold ocean under ice is a known habitat class on Earth only where energy arrives. Radiogenic and tidal heat are the budget. Prior for a present ocean: moderate to high on the geophysics. Prior for life in it: unknown, and lower than Mars, because the energy and the carbon path are less demonstrated. Emergence onto the face is not expected. The face is nitrogen ice.

## Ceres

**Face.** Dawn measured a dark, altered crust: magnesium phyllosilicates, ammoniated phyllosilicates, carbonates. Bright deposits in Occator crater are dominated by sodium carbonate, with ammonium carbonate or ammonium chloride. Models of those salts imply cold, alkaline brines, pH about $10$, salinity under about $100\,\mathrm{g}$ per kg of water.

**Interior.** The Occator deposits are read as residue of brines that reached the surface from below. Impact heat, or a brine still above its eutectic at depth, are both on the table. Ceres is a differentiated, water-rich body. A present deep brine is allowed by the data and not proven.

**Well.** This is the cleanest small-body case in the list. Aqueous alteration is not in dispute. A carbonate- and ammonium-rich brine is a sump chemistry. Prior for past liquid: high. Prior for a present brine at depth: moderate. Prior for life: unknown. The minerals raise the prior. They are not a cell.

---

## Vesta

**Face.** Dawn found a basaltic, differentiated surface consistent with the HED meteorites. Pyroxene-rich. A crust from igneous processing, not from a long aqueous career.

**Water.** Hydration is mostly delivered. Carbonaceous-chondrite-rich regions reach about $1000\,\mathrm{ppm}$ water. Background is about $100\,\mathrm{ppm}$, consistent with solar-wind implantation, retained because the surface is cold. This is not a hydrological system.

**Well.** No measured brine, no measured carbonate crust, no ocean model. Prior for a sump: low. Vesta is the control. A rocky body can be large, differentiated, and still never have offered the well.

## Moons and smaller bodies

The same prior. A buffered solvent under ice or rock beats a face open to a star or to a magnetosphere. The difference is that several moons have had the solvent sampled or strongly inferred. They are treated in the order of the evidence.

### Europa

**Evidence.** Galileo measured an induced magnetic field, read as a conductive saltwater layer under the ice. Chaos terrain and possible plume hints support a thin ice shell over a global ocean. Models put the ocean at something like two to three times Earth’s ocean volume, between ice and a rocky interior. Surface salts are consistent with Na, Mg, and Cl. Sulfates on the face may be ocean-sourced or made by radiolysis. Unsettled.

**Well.** A sump at moon scale. Tidal heat is the budget. A rocky seafloor is the catalyst. Prior for a present ocean: high. Prior for life: unknown. Emergence onto the face is not expected. The face is irradiated ice.

### Enceladus

**Evidence.** The strongest in the list. Cassini flew through south-polar plumes and measured them. Salty ice grains, H₂, CO₂, CH₄, NH₃, complex organics, and phosphorus at millimolar levels, orders of magnitude above Earth’s oceans. Silica grains point at hot water on rock. Libration says the ocean is global.

**Well.** A sampled sump. Hydrothermal indicators are the energy. Prior for a present ocean: high. Prior for a habitable chemistry: the highest in this file after Earth, because the ingredients were in an instrument. Prior for life: still unknown. A plume is not a cell.

### Ganymede

**Evidence.** Hubble auroral oscillations are read as a conductive layer. Interior models allow an ocean that may exceed Earth’s in volume, under a thick ice shell, above a silicate mantle and a liquid iron core. No plume has been sampled.

**Well.** A large, buffered ocean. Prior for the ocean: high on the magnetometry. Prior for life: unknown, and harder to reach than Europa. The shell is thick.

### Callisto

**Evidence.** An induced field from Galileo, later analyses strengthening the case. Geologically quiet. A probable ocean, not a confirmed one.

**Well.** If the ocean is there, it is old and low-energy. Prior: moderate. A quiet face is not evidence against a well. It is what a thick shell predicts.

### Titan

**Face.** A nitrogen atmosphere and a methane cycle. Lakes of hydrocarbons. That cycle is a solvent, and it is not water. A lower prior for assembly as we know it.

**Interior.** Gravity and tides were long read as a water–ammonia ocean. A 2025 reanalysis of Cassini tidal data favors a slushy interior with liquid pockets over a continuous ocean. Disputed.

**Well.** Two candidates, and they should not be merged. A hydrocarbon lake is a surface solvent of a different chemistry. A water pocket at depth is a well. Prior for the water pocket: now lower than it was. Prior for life in methane: speculative.

### Triton

**Evidence.** Voyager saw geysers and a young face. Thermal models allow a deep ocean under ice. No induced-field confirmation. No selected follow-up mission.

**Well.** Possible. Prior: low to moderate. A geyser is a path. It is not a sample of an ocean.

### Mimas, and the mid-sized Saturnian moons

Mimas, long read as inert, has an orbital drift that is fit by an ocean about $20\text{–}30\,\mathrm{km}$ under ice, possibly younger than $25$ million years. A young ocean is a young well. Dione and Rhea show past heat and possible past oceans. Tethys less so. Priors: Mimas moderate and recent. The others are past candidates.

### Earth’s Moon

Polar cold traps hold water ice. The crust is dry, gardened, and has no persistent liquid and no atmosphere to feed a well. A cold trap is a freezer, as on Mercury. Prior for a present well: low. Prior for a past, short-lived impact melt: possible, untestable as a habitat.

### Passed over

- **Io.** Intense volcanism and a sulfur face. No water ocean. A magma body is not the solvent this page uses. Prior: low.
- **Phobos and Deimos.** Dry, small, no hydrological system. Prior: low.
- **Most irregular and small moons.** No measured solvent, no measured heat budget that would hold one. Named here so the list is not silent. Not given a section.

| Venus | Possible early ocean | Unmeasured, surface sterilizing | Past: open. Now: low | Failed if it ever started |
| Earth | Yes | Yes, inhabited | Demonstrated habitat. Origin open | Happened |
| Mars | Yes, Noachian | Brine and maybe deep water | Plausible, unproven | Lower. Surface did not stay ready |
| Pluto | Ocean model | Ocean model | Unknown. Energy is the limit | Not expected |
| Ceres | Yes | Brine possible | Raised by salts. Unproven | Not expected |

| Europa | Ocean, strong | Ocean, strong | Unknown. Rocky seafloor | Not expected |

A single number for “life in the system” is refused. The split is the result. Assembly in a buffered sump is the prior. Emergence is a second, harder condition. Earth met both. Enceladus has had its chemistry in an instrument. Europa and Ganymede have the geophysics. Mars and Ceres keep a rocky-well prior. Vesta, the Moon, Io, and the small dry moons are the controls.

### What would move a row

- A cell, a lipid, or an isotopic biosignature in a brine or a fracture moves that body’s persistence to a fact.
- A long, sterile sample of the right chemistry lowers it.
- A resolved “no liquid” in the habitat that mattered lowers persistence. It does not rewrite a wet past.
- A demonstrated surface origin on Earth removes one analogy. It does not kill the Mars or Ceres prior.

## Open questions

- Did any terrestrial body assemble life in a sump, or only inherit it after a surface start?
- Is a brine vein on Mars wide enough, and fed enough, for a cell, or only for a model?
- Does Titan have a water pocket, a slush, or neither?
- Is Mimas’s ocean young, and does a young ocean count as a well or as a melt?
- What biosignature would this page accept from a plume that it would not accept from a model?

**Flip.** A swinging, irradiated surface shown to be the assembly site, paired with a sterile long-lived sump, flips the prior. A sterile sump under solvent, gradient, carbon, and time weakens the assembly claim. See the flip condition above.

These are the bets. They are not missing sections.

## Closing

A surface open to a star, still building its sky, is a hard place to start. A sump that already holds solvent, rock, and time is a better prior.

Earth shows that such places can be full of life. Earth does not show that they are where life began.

Of the planets, Mars and Ceres are the ones whose measured minerals and ices keep a rocky-well prior alive.

Of the moons, Enceladus has had its chemistry in an instrument, and Europa and Ganymede have the geophysics.

Pluto’s ocean is a geophysical bet. Mercury, Vesta, the Moon, and Io are poor wells. Venus is a past question under a present oven. The likelihood follows that split.

A trench, a drill, a plume, or a brine sample is how any one of those bets is called.

Further development, formalization, and testing are invited.
